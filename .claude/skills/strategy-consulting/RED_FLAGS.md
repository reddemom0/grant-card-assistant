# Red Flag Recognition: When to Say No

A critical consultant skill is knowing when something ISN'T a fit and being honest about it quickly. It saves everyone's time and builds long-term credibility. Use this to screen prospects, review intake notes, or pressure-test a "should we take this client?" question.

## Hard Disqualifiers

If any of these are present, the engagement (or the specific program play) is off the table as-is:

- **Pre-revenue with no co-funding capacity** — Can't sustain the cost-carry model. Redirect to R&D credits and free resources.
- **Single small need where fees would consume the grant** — One small student grant minus fees leaves nothing. Rightsize to a consultation.
- **No tangible project or plan** — Grants fund specific activities. "We might do something eventually" isn't enough to apply.
- **Activities already completed** — Cannot claim retroactively for most programs. Booked flights, signed contracts, or started work before applying → those expenses are likely ineligible.
- **Employee already on payroll** — Most hiring grants require applying BEFORE the hire. Can't retroactively fund someone already working. (Students have a narrow justification path — see `TIMING`.)
- **Under one year of operations** — Most programs require minimum one year from business registration. Give a specific re-engagement date, not a vague "come back later."
- **Sole proprietorship seeking innovation/export grants** — Not a hard no, but significantly fewer programs are accessible, and CanExport eligibility for sole proprietors is uncertain. Check internally before committing.
- **Company moving to co-packers** — Loses manufacturer classification. Critical discovery signal: "Do you make it yourself, or use a co-packer?" The answer reshapes the entire program set. If they're transitioning to co-packers, flag proactively — they may lose eligibility for programs they previously qualified for.
- **Middleman/reseller without Canadian value-add** — Buy, store, repack, re-export without substantial manufacturing = excluded from CanExport and most innovation programs. A planned value-add facility changes the picture — but only once operational.

## Soft Red Flags (Investigate Further)

- **Niche business between standard categories** — Event companies, recreational facilities, creative businesses. Be honest about what's available vs. not.
- **Monthly retainer-based vendor costs** — Grants require discrete projects with deliverables and end dates. Ongoing retainers don't qualify; the vendor relationship may need restructuring as a project.
- **Moving to co-packing** — Even as a plan, it changes the grant profile. Flag proactively.
- **High sophistication, low need** — A client who already applies successfully on their own may not see enough value in paid services. Platform subscription may be the right level.
- **Multi-entity complexity** — Multiple businesses, some with partners, some solo; each entity may have different eligibility. Don't assume — verify structure.
- **50%+ revenue from a single export market** — CanExport requires target markets to be <10% of revenue or <$100K. Heavy concentration in one market (especially the US) may disqualify their primary market. Redirect to secondary markets they haven't prioritized.
- **In-house training only** — BC ETG requires third-party training providers. If all training is internal, the ETG play shifts to adjacent skills (first aid, digital skills, leadership, business development) rather than core trade skills. Don't let the client believe their current approach qualifies when it doesn't.
- **Remote-only company seeking student hires** — Not a disqualifier, but some programs weigh the quality of the student experience, which is harder to demonstrate fully remote. Probe how they'd structure the placement.
- **Demographic funding misconceptions** — Clients often assume women-owned/youth-led/BIPOC-owned means a dedicated bucket of money. Reality: demographic factors typically increase subsidy rates within existing programs (70% instead of 50%, $7K instead of $5K) rather than opening separate programs. Manage expectations early.

## How to Deliver a "No"

- **Never just say no.** Pair it with what they CAN do, what would change the equation, or where else to look.
- **Be specific about why.** "Your revenue is below the threshold for this program" beats "you don't qualify."
- **Preserve the relationship.** "Right now this isn't a fit — but when X changes, come back and we'll build the strategy."
- **Rightsize rather than reject.** If Pro isn't right, maybe Starter is. If Starter isn't right, maybe a consultation covers it. (See `SIZING`.)

## How to Use This

When a consultant asks for a prospect screen:
1. Pull known facts (HubSpot record, intake notes, meeting transcripts)
2. Check every hard disqualifier explicitly — state which are clear, which fail, which are unknown
3. List triggered soft flags with the follow-up question that resolves each
4. If anything fails: draft the "no" (or "not yet") using the delivery principles, including the specific re-engagement trigger
