# Sizing: Matching Situation to Service

How to help a consultant rightsize a client — recommending the service level that actually makes sense for the client's situation, even when that means recommending less. Overselling destroys trust and leads to poor outcomes. Underselling leaves money on the table.

This file deliberately contains no prices, fee percentages, or promo codes — current pricing lives with the sales team and changes. Reason in tiers and ROI multiples; let the consultant attach current numbers.

## The Sizing Decision Tree

**Pre-revenue, no financial stability for co-funding:**
- NOT a fit for managed services at any tier
- Redirect to: SR&ED credits (if doing R&D), free database exploration, or a paid consultation when they're more established
- Plant the seed: "Once you have revenue established, come back and we can build a full strategy"
- Don't leave them feeling dismissed — validate the ambition, be specific about what changes the equation

**Under one year of operations:**
- Most programs require one year minimum from business registration date
- Route to free tier (database access, no support) to keep them in the ecosystem
- Give a specific date to re-engage: "Call me back in [month] when you've hit one year"
- If they're planning to hire before the one-year mark, they can start the hire now without a grant, then apply for the grant subsidy on the second term/renewal once eligible
- Referral potential: solo practitioners (bookkeepers, accountants) serving small businesses are natural referral partners — worth nurturing even when too small to be direct clients

**Revenue under ~$1-2M, limited grant needs:**
- Starter tier is the natural fit
- Includes a strategy session to build a roadmap + portal access for ongoing matching; ad hoc advisory for specific questions
- If they only have ONE small need (e.g., one student hire), even Starter may be too much — rightsize to a consultation instead
- Key test: will the annual fee + success fees leave enough of the grant value to make it worthwhile?

**Revenue $1-2M, high grant volume (10+ student hires or multi-entity):**
- Even below the typical Pro threshold, high application volume can justify Pro
- Key test: if Starter caps at 3 supported applications and they need 10+ placements, the math favors Pro (lower success-fee rate, unlimited applications)
- ProLite exists as a middle tier for companies that need more than Starter but can't justify full Pro
- Multi-entity owners: one subscription can cover multiple businesses. Multi-company packaging is handled case-by-case — check with the team for current arrangements

**Revenue $2M+, multiple growth initiatives:**
- Pro tier makes sense when there are multiple avenues to pursue simultaneously
- The client needs a dedicated team managing applications, claims, and strategy across programs
- Full managed service: account manager, researchers, coordinators, writers; success-based fees align incentives

**Experienced applicants who know what they're doing:**
- Even at $3-5M revenue, a competent client with successful past applications may not need Pro
- Starter + one-off writing services (like CanExport writing) can be more cost-effective
- The test: "Would you do this yourself if you had the information?" If yes → platform + targeted support. If no → managed service.

**Niche businesses that don't fully align with Granted's core:**
- Be honest. If only 20% of what the client needs falls within Granted's wheelhouse, say so
- Capture what IS a fit (student hiring is nearly universal), let go of what isn't
- Credibility comes from honesty, not from forcing a fit

## Sizing Principles

- **Start small and upgrade beats oversell and disappoint.** A client who starts on Starter and upgrades to Pro in year two is a better outcome than one who buys Pro, gets minimal ROI, and leaves.
- **Upgrade credits preserve the relationship.** Consultation fees credit toward annual subscriptions; Starter fees pro-rate into upgrades. Remind the consultant to mention this — it removes the risk of starting small.
- **Do the math out loud.** If the annual fee plus success fees would consume most of the grant value the client can realistically capture, acknowledge that the math is tight. Don't hide it.
- **Nine times out of ten, the answer to "should I get Pro?" is no.** Most businesses are better served by Starter + targeted support. Saying so builds credibility.
- **10x ROI is the Pro qualification heuristic.** If there's no visible path to grant value of at least 10x the Pro investment, recommend Starter instead. This is Jorge's explicit threshold: "When I'm looking to work with a company, 10x is my minimum."
- **Proactively downgrade at renewal.** If a Pro client's circumstances change and they can't leverage 10x, move them to Starter at renewal rather than letting them overpay. Protecting the client's ROI protects the relationship.
- **The revenue threshold for Pro is situational, not absolute.** It depends on grant volume potential, not just revenue. A $1.7M company hiring 10 students justifies Pro; a $3M company with one small need doesn't. Let application volume drive the tier.
- **"We don't send materials" — route to the product.** When prospects ask to "just send a list of grants," the answer is no: grants change constantly. Route them to the Starter subscription as the always-current reference. Don't give away free deliverables that go stale.

## The Client Lifecycle View

Tiers aren't just packages — they're a lifecycle:

- **Free tier** → Database access, no support. For prospects not ready for any paid tier (pre-revenue, under one year, too small). Keeps them in the ecosystem; good for referral partners.
- **Starter** → Self-serve with a capped number of supported applications. For companies under ~$2M with limited grant needs, or experienced applicants who want the intelligence layer but can execute themselves.
- **ProLite** → Middle tier for companies that need more than Starter's cap but can't justify full Pro. A natural step for mid-range clients after ~6 months on Starter.
- **Pro** → Full managed service: dedicated account manager, research team, coordinators, writers. For companies with 10x ROI potential. Quarterly strategy meetings minimum, weekly notifications, proactive monitoring.
- **Movement in both directions:** upgrades carry credits; downgrades at renewal protect over-served clients. Both preserve the relationship.

## How to Use This

When a consultant asks "what tier should [client] be on":
1. Pull the client's facts (revenue, entity structure, grant history from HubSpot; application volume potential from their plans)
2. Walk the decision tree top-down; note which branch fires and why
3. Sanity-check with the 10x heuristic and the "would they do it themselves?" test
4. Output the recommendation with its reasoning AND the honest counter-case (what would make the next tier up or down correct), so the consultant can defend it in the room
