# Timing as a Strategic Lever

Timing is one of the most powerful tools in grant strategy. The same company with the same needs can get dramatically different results depending on WHEN they apply, WHEN they start spending, and WHEN they file their tax returns. Use this to advise consultants on timing questions and to timing-check any roadmap (see `SEQUENCING`).

## Universal Timing Principles

**Apply before you spend.** The single most important timing rule across almost all programs. Once money is committed (booked flights, signed contracts, deposits), those expenses often become ineligible. The sequence is: apply → get approved → then execute and spend. Rare exceptions exist (some retroactive programs), but the default is always: don't spend before the grant is in place.

**Earlier applications get faster decisions.** Programs adjudicate roughly in order of receipt. February applications may be approved by April; May applications might not be approved until November. Same program, same quality — different timing.

**Government fiscal year drives everything.** The Canadian federal fiscal year starts April 1. New programs launch, existing programs get new funding tranches, rules may change. The weeks before and after April 1 are critical planning periods.

**Programs change mid-cycle.** Don't assume last year's rules apply this year — rules can change AFTER a program opens. Staying current on changes is a core piece of Granted's value (check `get_visualping_alerts` and run `grants/validation` before the consultant commits to specifics).

**Build readiness before windows open.** When a program is closed but expected to reopen, use the downtime to prepare: documents ready, job descriptions written, market research done. When the window opens, be first in line.

## Timing Tactics

- **Fiscal year-end strategy:** If a startup is approaching a revenue threshold (e.g., $100K for export program eligibility), a strategic short fiscal year-end can realize that revenue on their T2 sooner, unlocking programs months earlier.
- **Semester alignment:** Student hiring programs run on academic semesters. Hiring in July? It may be better to start the grant placement in September for a full term of coverage rather than catching the tail of the summer term.
- **Re-application timing:** If a student works out, reapply for subsequent terms — update the job description to show incremental responsibilities. This is the documented re-application requirement, and it can stack a single student across multiple terms.
- **Early deposit warnings:** Trade shows and events dangle early-bird discounts on booths and next-year deposits. If deposits are made before grant approval — and the program requires being "reliant on the grant" — the expense becomes ineligible. Some programs (like CanExport) can have pre-approval expenses written in, but the safe default: don't commit funds until timing is clear. If they MUST book before approval: reimbursement may still work if the grant lands, but rejection means out-of-pocket.
- **End-of-fiscal awareness:** Late February through March is the thinnest period for program availability — most programs have run out of funds or are winding down. After April 1, expect a wave of new programs and renewed funding. Use this to explain why pickings are slim in March and to set expectations for April.
- **Training grant fiscal boundary:** Training happening before April must be applied for under the current fiscal year — apply immediately. Training after April waits for the new fiscal year to open. Missing this boundary means missing the funding entirely.
- **Students already on payroll:** Can still be grant-eligible, but the application must justify placing them under a student hiring program retroactively. Better to align start dates with program cycles (April for summer term). "It's okay they're already working for you, but you have to justify them being under a student program when you started them two months before."

## How to Use This

When a consultant asks a timing question:
1. Identify the commitment at risk (spend, hire start, training start, deposit, fiscal filing)
2. Apply the relevant principle/tactic and state the safe default plus any documented exception
3. Verify current program status and deadlines before the consultant relays dates (`grants/validation`, `get_visualping_alerts`, `search_getgranted`)
4. Timing-check the whole roadmap while you're at it: anything the client is about to spend on, any window opening within 90 days, any fiscal boundary approaching
