# Strategic Sequencing: Prioritizing and Layering Opportunities

When a client has multiple potential grant opportunities (most do), the consultant's job is a sequenced roadmap — not a laundry list. Order matters: bandwidth is limited, timing windows are real, and early wins build momentum. Use this to help a consultant build or sanity-check a roadmap.

## The Priority Framework

**1. Time-sensitive opportunities first.** Is a program about to close? Did something just open? Is the client about to make a spending decision that would disqualify them? Urgency overrides everything else.

**2. Highest dollar value.** Export grants at $25-50K typically dwarf individual hiring grants at $5-7K. If the client qualifies for both, the bigger opportunity deserves more attention — but don't ignore the smaller wins.

**3. Easiest wins.** Training grants for courses they're already taking. Hiring grants for candidates they've already identified. Minimal effort, and they build confidence in the process — especially for first-time applicants.

**4. Stackable opportunities.** Can they get both a federal hiring grant AND a provincial one for the same employee? That doubles the value with marginal extra effort. Always check whether programs allow stacking.

**5. Pipeline building.** Even if a program isn't open right now, help the client prepare so they're ready when it opens. "This program opens in April — let's have your materials ready in March."

## Sequencing Patterns from Real Consultations

Named patterns to match against. When a client resembles one, start from its sequence and adapt:

**Urgency-first (tariff-impacted manufacturer):**
Time-sensitive relief fund → parallel export application → ongoing hiring grants → training as steady-state

**Multi-avenue layering (growing consumer brand):**
Student hiring grants (immediate, easy) → export grant (bigger value, more planning) → training subsidies (ongoing) → R&D credits (longer-term)

**Downsell sequencing (pre-revenue startup):**
R&D tax credits (most relevant now) → free database access (self-serve) → paid consultation (when established)

**Single-need rightsizing (solo operator, one hire):**
One consultation → everything needed for one application → upgrade credit preserved for later

**Dual-stream parallel (company with both export and innovation needs):**
Two different streams of the same program running simultaneously for different markets/activities → hiring grants as parallel layer → timing pressure dictates order

**Specialist referral (outside core expertise):**
Identify the mismatch → pivot to adjacent opportunity within expertise → refer to specialist for the core need → preserve relationship for what IS a fit

**Capacity-constrained high-growth (CPG brand scaling fast):**
"Don't try to get everything now. You have big growth goals and limits on capacity. This one, then this one, then this one." → CanExport for immediate market entry → digital adoption/technology assessment (when program reopens) → student hiring as capacity allows → training ongoing

**Forestry seasonal ramp-up:**
Training grants for immediate certifications (first aid, silviculture) → student hiring programs for summer season (April openings) → Canada Summer Jobs for seasonal labor → post-graduate green jobs placements → CanExport only if international markets are in play

**Two-company owner (tech startup + established family business):**
Map each entity separately → established business: ETG training + student hiring (immediate ROI) → startup: tech student hiring + innovation programs (when past MVP) → CanExport for whichever entity has international plans → one subscription covers both

## The Golden Rule of Sequencing

**Don't try to do everything at once.** Even if five programs apply, the client has limited bandwidth. Pick the top two or three, execute those well, then layer on the next round. A scattered approach leads to weak applications and overwhelmed clients.

## The Annual Strategy Rhythm

Grant strategy is a cycle, not an event. Roadmaps should assume:

1. **Annual strategy meeting** — Deep review of business goals, identification of all applicable programs, creation of a prioritized roadmap for the year. Quarterly for managed-service clients.
2. **Ongoing monitoring** — Matched grant notifications land weekly; new programs launch, criteria change, funding tranches open and close. Clients need to stay engaged, not set-and-forget.
3. **Trigger-based action** — Whenever a client is hiring, starting training, planning expansion, or making a significant business change, check for applicable grants. The best consultants train clients to flag changes BEFORE they happen.
4. **Application and claims management** — For managed clients, the consultant handles prep, submission, follow-up, and claims. For self-serve clients, the platform and advisory support them through it.

What clients need internalized (worth building into any roadmap handoff):
- **Forward government communications immediately** — within 2 business days; delayed responses can get grants pulled.
- **Apply before starting** — especially training grants; even one day late is too late.
- **Plans change, and that's okay** — a quit hire, cancelled course, or shifted expansion needs immediate notification so adjustments can be made. Waiting until the funding period ends creates problems.
- **Grants are a long game** — year-over-year engagement captures dramatically more funding than apply-once-and-walk-away.

## How to Use This

When a consultant asks for a roadmap:
1. Start from identified programs (run `grants/matching` + `grants/validation` first if the program set isn't established)
2. Score each opportunity against the 5-part priority framework; check stacking
3. Match the client to the nearest named pattern(s) and adapt
4. Cap the active lane at 2-3 items; everything else goes to a dated "next round" or pipeline-prep lane
5. Output: a sequenced roadmap with rationale per step, trigger points, and what the client must not do in the meantime (spending commitments — see `TIMING`)
