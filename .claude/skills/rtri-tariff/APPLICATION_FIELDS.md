# RTRI — Application Fields

Every field in the RTRI application, with its prompt, character limit, and what
PacifiCan's guidance says to cover. Loaded by the WRITEUP path.

Program facts — eligibility, caps, dates, cost rules — live in
`PROGRAM_FACTS`. This file is the form itself.

**Sources:** PacifiCan Applicant Guide for Businesses (modified 2026-09-14);
completed application form; RTRI Supplemental Form (August 2025).

---

## ⚠️ Do not import from the older form template

A circulating `.docx` version of the application form is out of date. It shows
retroactivity from March 21, 2025, an indicator table with HQP jobs, non-HQP
jobs, incremental private sector investment, and cost-reduction technologies,
and no liquidity assistance section at all.

All three are wrong. Current retroactivity is in `PROGRAM_FACTS`, the current
indicators are below, and liquidity is a live stream. If a field's wording
looks different from this file, this file wins.

## Tab 1 — Organization Information

Mostly factual fields: legal name, operating name, addresses, contact details,
Indigenous organization status, corporate status, organization type, CRA
business number, jurisdiction and date of incorporation, FTE count.

**FTE count** — full-time equivalents, including affiliated companies. A
part-time employee at ~20 hrs/week counts as 0.5 FTE.

**Organization description — 500 characters**
> Describe your organization and explain its mandate and priorities.

Cover: primary products and services; markets served; company history and
growth; competitive advantage and competition; the date the organization
established business in Canada; whether it's a subsidiary of another company.

Tight for what's being asked. Lead with what the business makes and for whom,
then size and history.

---

## Tab 2 — Project Information

Primary and secondary contacts. The primary contact is who PacifiCan follows
up with.

**Project title — 90 characters**
> Provide a project title in plain language that accurately reflects the
> activities and results of the project.

Disclosed publicly on the Open Government Portal if funded. For a
liquidity-only application the title can be "Liquidity Assistance for
[Company Name]".

**Project activities — 1,000 characters**
> Describe your project activities in plain language. The description should
> include the objectives of the project, as well as key activities and how
> these activities will achieve the intended objectives.

Cover: main steps or actions; who will be involved; where activities take
place; when they will generally happen. List research, planning, construction,
or training components if present. Explain why funding is sought, how funds
will be used, and how they support the objectives. **No technical language.**

Liquidity-only: briefly state how liquidity assistance will allow operations
to be maintained.

**Economic benefits — 1,000 characters**
> Briefly describe the economic benefits associated with this project.

Cover: clear and tangible direct and indirect economic outcomes; who benefits
— Indigenous communities, youth, newcomers, rural residents; how the outcomes
were identified and what assumptions were relied on; how outcomes will be
tracked and reported.

Inclusive growth is a stated PacifiCan investment priority. Results must be
measurable and directly attributable, reportable during the project and up to
one year past the end date.

Liquidity-only: state the number of jobs and kinds of economic activity that
will be maintained.

**RDA Priority — dropdown, choose one**

Options align to: market diversification; supply chain improvements;
consideration and use of Canadian technology and products; expansion of
inter-provincial trade and supply chains.

⚠️ Businesses in aluminum, automotive, copper, food manufacturing, forestry, or
steel — or experiencing tariff impacts primarily as a result of tariffs in one
of those sectors — **select "Other."** Per the guide, "This will ensure
applications from businesses in these industries are easily identified." Any
sector may be eligible; the list only identifies files.

Prior to Sept 8, 2026 the list was forestry, metal, machinery and equipment,
and agriculture and seafood. Don't use the old list.

**Explain how the project supports the indicated priority — 2,000 characters**

Align objectives, activities, and outcomes to the priorities above. If "Other"
was selected, identify the industry here.

**Proposed start and end dates** — see `PROGRAM_FACTS` for the stream-specific
windows. Liquidity and pivot differ.

## Tab 3 — Funding

**Project costs table** — description, type (capital or non-capital), amount.

Each cost item on its own line, summarized by type of cost (e.g. salaries)
rather than by activity. For salaries, list the positions and number of
employees funded. Travel must comply with the Treasury Board of Canada
Secretariat Travel Directive. All costs supported by reasonable estimates.

Liquidity-only: list salaries and essential operating costs.

**Cash flows** — one row per fiscal year, with total fiscal expenditures, RDA
funding requested, and non-RDA funding (auto-calculated).

PacifiCan's fiscal year runs April 1 to March 31. Total expenditures must
match total project costs. Total non-RDA funding must match the funding
partners total.

> Estimate cash flows carefully — changes will not automatically be approved.

**Funding partners** — first row is the applicant's own contribution. Add all
other monetary contributors, including any anticipated support from other
Government of Canada tariff response programs such as EI Work-Sharing.

In-kind contributions are not listed here.

**Project partnerships — 1,200 characters**
> Briefly describe any project partnerships, financial and non-financial,
> including any costs covered.

Cover: the management team, board, advisors, and partnerships important to the
project's success. If awaiting confirmation on financial partnerships, explain
when a decision is expected.

## Tab 4 — Benefits

**Primary activity — choose one.** Productivity improvement; market expansion
and diversification; strengthening supply chains and trade resilience. The
project may be compared against similar projects in that activity.

**Indicator table** — a target and target date for each. Enter "0" and a date
where there's no value. Targets are incremental results, from funding start to
up to one year after the proposed end date.

| Indicator | Definition |
|---|---|
| Revenue growth ($) | Total increased revenues within Canada resulting from the project |
| Export sales growth ($) | Total anticipated sales growth related to exports |
| Number of jobs created | FTE jobs that did not exist before and were created as a result. One FTE = 12 months full-time, typically 40 hrs/week. Convert seasonal and part-time |
| Number of jobs maintained | Jobs that existed before the project but would not continue or would likely be lost without liquidity assistance |
| Number of technologies to market | Knowledge-based products, processes, services, or technologies meeting legal and regulatory requirements for sale |
| Value of Canadian content used | Canadian value-added in goods, services, or labour. Services: work performed by people based in Canada. Goods: value added in Canada — assembly, customization, installation, integration, training, commissioning, support |
| Value of Canadian materials used | Steel, wood products, and aluminum **produced and manufactured in Canada** only. Produced in Canada means primary manufacturing and fabrication happened here |

**Jobs maintained excludes:** contractors; vacant positions; future hires;
employees elsewhere in Canada outside this RDA's catchment; employees outside
Canada; payroll covered by another government program, including jobs
maintained through an RTRI pivot project.

It must not exceed the total number of Canadian employees entered in
Organization Information (Tab 1), must be current as of the
application date, and must be consistent with the payroll information
supplied.

Liquidity-only: focus on the accuracy of jobs maintained.

**Indicator detail — 3,000 characters**
> Provide details on how each of the above indicators will be achieved and
> tracked, as well as any other significant economic benefits.

Cover: how each outcome will be achieved; the assumptions behind the
estimates; long-term plans beyond the funding period; how benefits continue
after the initial investment; mechanisms or partnerships supporting
continuation or scaling. Non-economic benefits can go here too.

> All projects will be screened for the significance of outcomes and their
> likelihood of achievement.

Liquidity-only: describe how you will identify which jobs are maintained as a
result of the funding.

**Francophone benefit — yes/no, then 1,500 characters**
> Describe how the project activities will benefit Francophones.

Select yes if activities will directly benefit the Francophone community in
Western Canada, **or if you are willing to modify the project** to extend
benefits to them. Explain what specific activity or activities will or could
be included. If there's no targeted impact, select no and write "not
applicable."

**Alignment with other priorities — 1,500 characters**

Whether the project aligns with other Government of Canada priorities,
provincial government priorities, industry needs, and the organization's
long-term research, capital, or strategic plans. Explain alignment with
industry and market demand in the community.

## Tab 5 — Timelines

**Full project description — 3,000 characters**
> Describe your project.

Cover: the objectives; key activities and how they contribute to those
objectives; the plan to achieve them; engagement or commitments from other
partners such as industry or other levels of government; project risks and
mitigation measures — delays, staff turnover, other challenges.

Liquidity-only: enter "not applicable."

**Milestone table** — milestone and date of expected completion (YYYY-MM-DD).

Significant, clearly defined, measurable activities that can be tracked over
the course of the project, up to completion.

Liquidity-only: the timeline cannot exceed 12 months. Quarterly milestones
demonstrating maintenance of operations are acceptable.

**Timeline comments — 1,000 characters**

Clarify the milestones. Enter "not applicable" if nothing needs clarifying.
Useful for explaining gaps between acquisition and commissioning, phased
approvals, or costs that fall outside the eligible window.

---

## Tab 6 — Market

> In this section, we are looking for a description of how your project is
> distinct from your normal operations.

**Are other organizations providing a similar activity, service, or product?**
Yes or no.

**How your activity, service, or product differs — 1,000 characters**

Cover: years in the target market or sector; major competitors; how the
project affects competitive position or market share in Canada and
internationally; whether the project will adversely impact other businesses in
Canada.

**Rationale for the project and RDA involvement — 2,000 characters**

This question is optional per the guide, but it's where incrementality gets
argued. Cover: the opportunity or challenge and how the project solves it; the
rationale for government's role — why can't they do this alone, and why now;
how the project is incremental to current operations.

Liquidity-only: may be left blank.

**Which western provinces benefit** — select all that apply. PacifiCan only
supports projects with benefits in British Columbia.

**Will the project generate revenues?** Yes or no. If yes — 1,000 characters
on how much, and what the revenue will be used for.

---

## Tab 7 — Management

**Governance and oversight — 2,000 characters**
> Clearly demonstrate that your organization has effective governance measures
> in place to execute this project. Indicate how oversight will be provided.

Cover: how the organization is governed — board of directors, advisory board —
and its structure; key executives' credentials, including years of experience;
how these individuals will specifically provide oversight on this project.

Liquidity-only: focus on how the organization will ensure funding supports
continuation of operations.

**Key individuals — 2,000 characters**
> Describe the qualifications and related experience of the key individuals
> responsible for managing and implementing the project.

Cover: who will directly manage and implement; their prior experience,
education, professional designations, achievements. Demonstrate the management
skills and leadership to carry out the project.

Liquidity-only: a list of individuals involved in administering funding is
sufficient.

## Tab 8 — Additional Details

Factual fields: date the organization established business in Canada; whether
it's a subsidiary and of whom; whether it's a manufacturer, supplier, or
value-added business in a tariff-impacted sector; **fiscal year end date**;
total revenues from the most recent and previous fiscal year ends; whether it
has previously received RDA funding; whether an independent market assessment
or evidence of unmet demand exists; where competitors operate; a description
of the competition; whether it has operating facilities in Western Canada.

**Most significant risk and mitigation — 1,000 characters**

Key risks — financial, trade, regulatory, implementation — plus potential risk
to the broader economy such as cybersecurity or privacy, and the mitigation
measures in place.

**Tariff impact — select all that apply.** The list is in `PROGRAM_FACTS`.

> Select only impacts that you can explain in the following question,
> supported with evidence.

**Tariff impact narrative — 2,000 characters**
> Describe how tariffs, or the uncertainty they have created, have affected
> your business.

Cover: what changed and when; which products, materials, customers, suppliers,
or markets were affected; the financial or operational impact; how the impact
affects the ability to maintain operations or employment in Canada; the
evidence that can be provided.

Evidence uploads go in Tab 9 and may include export sales records showing
reduced volumes; invoices, supplier notices, or purchase orders showing
increased costs; import or export tax on products; customs documents; revenue
decline from lost customers or contracts; layoffs, hiring freezes, or EI
Work-Sharing uptake.

> You do not need to attach every business record. Provide enough for
> PacifiCan to understand and verify the tariff-related impact.

**This is the foundation of the application.** Specific figures and dates
belong here, and the same figures should recur consistently in the project
rationale, economic benefits, and market sections.

**Type of support requested** — select all that apply. The three streams are
in `PROGRAM_FACTS`.

### Liquidity questions — liquidity applicants only

Skip entirely if not requesting liquidity.

**Total liquidity assistance requested (up to $2M).**
> Base your request on your demonstrated liquidity shortfall over the next 12
> months and the amount required to maintain operations and employment in
> Canada.

Supported by eligible payroll and, where necessary, essential operating costs,
after accounting for forecasted revenue, available financing, working capital,
and other government assistance. **Approved amounts may be less than
requested.**

**Average monthly payroll, most recent 12 months.** Eligible payroll for the
most recent 12 completed months ÷ 12. Inclusions and exclusions are in
`PROGRAM_FACTS`.

**Other recurring operating costs, most recent 12 months.** Only rent or
commercial lease, utilities, business insurance, property taxes. Exclusions
are in `PROGRAM_FACTS`.

**How assistance maintains employment and operations, and how the gap gets
covered — 2,000 characters**

Cover: the cash flow position over the funding period against pre-tariff
baselines, as demonstrated by the cash flow forecast; how assistance helps
meet payroll, cover short-term shortages, manage seasonal fluctuations, or
fund working capital; what could happen without it. Connect the amount
requested to specific payroll periods and jobs, working capital availability,
cash flow forecasts, or loan agreements.

**Liquidity attestation** — separate from the main attestation. Only liquidity
applicants agree to it.

## Tab 9 — Documents

The full required and optional document lists are in `PROGRAM_FACTS`.

Portal upload categories: confirmation of funding; current financial
statements; forecasted income statements and cash flows; incorporation
documents; project plan; recent financial statements (2 years); supporting
documents.

> Do not provide a Social Insurance Number on any documentation.

## Tab 10 — Diversity and Inclusion

Optional. Not used to determine eligibility; collected for statistical
purposes.

Whether the organization is majority (over 50%) owned or led by individuals
self-identifying in listed groups, and whether the project directly supports
any of them. If yes to any — 1,500 characters explaining how.

Leave blank rather than guessing.

---

## Tab 11 — Signing Authority

Contact details for the person submitting, who must have signing power and
authority to enter a legal agreement. May be different from the project
contact.

**Tab 12 — Submit** unlocks only when all other tabs are complete. Submitting
means the signing authority is agreeing to the attestation.

## Supplemental Form (Annex IV)

Required for businesses with commercial projects. The application form's
attestation covers it.

**1. Legal name of applicant organization** — must match the application.

**2. Canadian technologies and products — 3,000 characters**

Answer each part:
- (A) Did the business previously use Canadian technologies and products in
  past investment projects or current production?
- (B) For the proposed project, are there opportunities to use Canadian
  technologies and products in implementation or production?
- (B.1) If yes to B, did you consider using them for this project?
- (B.1.a) If yes to B.1, what proportion of total eligible costs is associated
  with Canadian technologies and products?
- (C) If no to A or B, share why they weren't implemented before and why no
  opportunity is seen today — for example, no Canadian manufacturer or
  supplier exists — and/or explain the reason for not choosing them, such as
  prohibitive cost.

This is the Buy Canadian Policy question and it's assessed. A weak or skipped
answer is a missed opportunity; an honest "no Canadian equivalent exists at
our specification" is a legitimate answer when true.

**3. Economic benefits and supply chain role — 3,000 characters**
*Non-repayable funding requests only.*

Describe how the business: (a) generates economic benefits for the local
economy or region — employment, consolidation of the economic base, creation
of added value, role in the value chain; and (b) plays an important role in
supporting the local supply chain, with target markets that may be regional,
national, or international.

---

## Attestation Form (Annex III)

A PDF requiring a **wet signature** — physically signed by hand in ink,
scanned, and attached. The original is retained by the applicant.

Signed by someone with legal authority to enter a contribution agreement on
behalf of the organization. This surfaces late and delays submissions — raise
it early.
