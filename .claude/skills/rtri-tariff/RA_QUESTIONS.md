# RTRI — RA Question Bank

The readiness assessment questions, as Granted asks them. Load during Step 6
of the READINESS path.

These are the questions themselves. Eligibility thresholds, dates, caps, and
cost rules live in `PROGRAM_FACTS` — load that too, and never state a program
fact from this file that isn't there.

**Use the wording.** These questions are shaped by what PacifiCan actually
asks and what the team has learned gets useful answers. Rephrase for flow, not
for substance.

**Skip what's already answered.** The person running the RA has usually spoken
to the client. Ask about gaps.

---

## Project Overview — all streams

**Briefly describe your project in plain language.**

Get: the main steps or actions the organization will take; who will be
involved; where activities take place; when they'll generally happen.

For Pivot, this becomes the Project Activities section — max 1,000 characters.
For Liquidity, it's a brief statement of how liquidity assistance will let
operations be maintained.

**Explain how the project will fill a market gap or an industry need.**

Get: the opportunity or challenge, and how the project solves it. The
rationale for government's role — why can't the client do this alone, and why
now? How the project is incremental to current operations, not a continuation
of what they're already doing.

**Competitive landscape.**

Are competitors doing something similar? If so, how is this client's approach
different or better? This helps PacifiCan understand market positioning.

---

## Project-type specific

Ask only the set that fits.

**Productivity or efficiency projects:**
- Walk through the current process — how long does it take today?
- How much time or cost would the new approach save?
- What other measures show productivity improvement? Throughput, quality,
  accuracy, waste reduction.

**Market expansion projects:**
- What type of companies are you targeting?
- What market data is driving this expansion decision?
- How would success in this market support your overall growth strategy?

---

## Project Scope & Timeline — Pivot

- Does the project include research, planning, construction, training, or
  other components?
- Have you already started? If yes, note which costs fall inside the eligible
  retroactive window. If no, when will you start?
- When will the project be completed?

Check the retroactivity dates in `PROGRAM_FACTS` against what they tell you.
Costs in the excluded window are a common and expensive misunderstanding.

---

## Project Activities & Expenses — Pivot

**What are the key activities and expenses?**

Categorize by type: labour (wages and benefits for project-specific staff);
capital (machinery, equipment, infrastructure, technology); consulting
(professional and technical services); market development (trade shows,
marketing materials, sales activities, distributor partnerships); other (legal
or regulatory requirements).

Training is an open item — see `PROGRAM_FACTS`. Don't budget it as eligible.

---

## Project Objectives & Outcomes — Pivot

- What are the key objectives of the project?
- How will these activities achieve those objectives?

Clients should be able to speak to outcomes in the indicator areas:
revenue growth from increased Canadian sales; export sales growth; net new FTE
positions; new technology or processes implemented; value of Canadian content
used; value of Canadian materials used.

**How will these activities achieve the program's objectives?**

Remind them what the program is for: defending Canadian jobs, industries, and
supply chains from tariff disruptions. The project should connect clearly to
at least one.

---

## Economic Benefits & Impact — Pivot

**What are the tangible economic benefits?**

Direct: revenue growth, cost savings, jobs created, supply chain resilience.
Indirect: supplier purchasing volumes, community economic activity, regional
growth.

Inclusive growth is prioritized — does the project create opportunities for
Indigenous communities, youth, newcomers, or rural residents?

**How will you track and report on these outcomes?**

Get: measurement methodology; the key metrics they'll track; the reporting
timeline, during the project and up to one year after completion.

---

## Tariff Impact Narrative — all streams

**The critical section.** A 2,000-character narrative, and the foundation of
both Pivot and Liquidity applications.

**Describe how tariffs have affected your business.**

Gather, with specifics:

- **What changed and when** — which tariff types, announcement dates, when the
  impact was felt
- **What was affected** — specific products, materials, customers, suppliers,
  markets
- **Financial and operational impact** — revenue decline percentage, margin
  compression, volume reduction, cost increases
- **Effect on Canadian operations and employment** — risk of layoffs, facility
  closure, market share loss
- **Evidence available** — export sales records, supplier notices, customs
  documents, financial records

Push for numbers and dates. "Significantly affected" is not an answer — a
percentage, a dollar figure, or a named lost customer is. This section is
where weak applications are made.

## Liquidity-specific

**Why does your business need liquidity assistance right now?**
- Describe the cash flow shortfall caused by tariffs
- How does the current cash position compare to the pre-tariff baseline?
- Which specific payroll periods or operating costs are at risk?

**How will liquidity assistance help you maintain operations?**
Meeting payroll obligations, covering short-term shortfalls, managing seasonal
fluctuations, funding working capital.

**What could happen without the assistance?**
Risk of layoffs or closure? Loss of key customers or contracts? Inability to
meet supplier obligations?

**How will you cover the gap between assistance and total operational needs?**
Available financing — unused line of credit, investor capital, retained
earnings? Revenue forecasts or cost reductions? Other government assistance
such as EI Work-Sharing?

---

## Funding & Co-Contribution — Pivot

**How are you paying for the client contribution?**

Retained earnings or cash reserves? Unused line of credit? Investor capital or
equity? Project partner contributions? Other government assistance?

The percentage split is an open item — see `PROGRAM_FACTS`. Ask how they're
funding their share without stating what the required share is.

What counts as proof, and what doesn't, is in `PROGRAM_FACTS`. Forecasted
revenues and accounts receivable are not accepted, and clients are often
surprised by that.

---

## Questions worth asking that aren't on the form

These come from what the team has learned, not from PacifiCan:

- **Have you applied to other tariff response programs?** Other federal
  support has to be disclosed as a funding partner, and payroll covered
  elsewhere can't be claimed here.
- **Who has signing authority?** The attestation needs a wet signature from
  someone with authority to enter a contribution agreement. This surfaces late
  and delays submissions.
- **Are your financials externally reviewed, and how recent?** Two complete
  years, prepared by an external accountant, plus current interim statements.
- **Does a project plan exist, or does one need building?** It's mandatory for
  Pivot applications.
