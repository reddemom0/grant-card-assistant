# CanExport SME Competitive Applications Guide (2026-27)

## Overview

CanExport SMEs is **competitive** and funding is **NOT guaranteed**. Applications are assessed on **rolling basis** while funding remains available. Meeting minimum eligibility requirements does not ensure funding approval.

**Assessment Focus**:
- Feasibility of proposed activities
- Adherence to program guidelines
- Alignment with program objectives and Government's trade diversification goals

---

## What Makes Applications Competitive

### 1. Demonstrate Readiness and Focus

**Competitive applications clearly describe**:

**Why Ready Now**:
- Existing export experience (if any)
- Internal capacity to execute proposed activities
- Strategic rationale for international expansion timing
- Proven domestic sales and production capacity
- Market research already completed

**Market Fit**:
- **Why these specific markets** align with company's product/service
- Evidence of opportunity in selected markets
- Clear understanding of market requirements and entry barriers
- Realistic assessment of market potential

**Measurable Outcomes**:
- **How activities will achieve concrete export results**
- Specific, quantifiable objectives (not vague aspirations)
- Timeline for expected outcomes
- Success metrics beyond "increased sales"

**Market Linkage**:
- **Every activity linked to specific target market** with clear rationale
- Logical connection between market research findings and proposed activities
- Activities form coherent sequence toward market entry
- No standalone or disconnected tasks

**⚠️ Red Flags (Readiness Concerns)**:
- Vague market selection ("exploring opportunities")
- No prior export experience mentioned
- Unclear internal capacity to execute
- Activities not linked to specific markets
- Unrealistic timelines or outcomes
- No evidence of market research
- Requesting funding for exploratory activities that should be completed before applying

---

### 2. Show Clear Alignment with Program Objectives

**Strong Applications Demonstrate**:

**Logical Sequence**:
- Market research → Selected activities → Desired outcomes
- Each step builds on previous step
- Clear progression toward market entry
- Activities timed appropriately (e.g., research before trade show attendance)

**Strategic Coherence**:
- Each activity supports international business development strategy
- Activities complement each other
- No redundant or contradictory activities
- Budget allocation reflects strategic priorities

**Purpose Clarity**:
- Applicant understands **why** each expense matters
- Can articulate **how** each activity contributes to export objectives
- Demonstrates understanding of eligible vs. ineligible costs
- Shows awareness of program requirements and limitations

**Integrated Plan**:
- Activities form **coherent market development plan**, not standalone tasks
- Clear narrative connecting all proposed activities
- Demonstrates understanding of market entry process
- Shows progression from initial contact through to sales

**⚠️ Red Flags (Alignment Issues)**:
- Activities appear random or disconnected
- No clear strategy linking activities together
- Expenses listed without strategic justification
- Budget doesn't align with stated objectives
- Requesting activities that don't support stated goals
- Mixing different market entry strategies without clear rationale

---

### 3. Provide Complete and Specific Information

**Avoid Vague Descriptions**. Competitive applications include:

**Specific Target Markets**:
- Name each country (not regions or continents)
- Explain **why each market selected** based on:
  - Market size and growth potential
  - Competitive landscape
  - Regulatory environment
  - Existing trade relationships
  - Company's competitive advantage in that market
  - Alignment with company's export strategy

**Detailed Activity Descriptions**:
- **What** will be done
- **Where** it will take place (destination vs. target market)
- **When** it will occur (realistic timeline)
- **Who** will participate (travelers, consultants, partners)
- **Why** it's necessary for market entry
- **Expected outcomes** from each activity

**Realistic Budgets**:
- Line-item breakdown of costs
- Justification for each expense
- Evidence of market research on typical costs
- Appropriate cost estimates (not inflated or underestimated)
- Clear distinction between eligible and company-funded costs

**Demonstrated Understanding**:
- Shows knowledge of eligible vs. ineligible costs
- Correctly categorizes expenses
- Understands program rules (U.S. exclusivity, sector exclusions, etc.)
- Provides required documentation and detail

**Examples of Specificity**:

❌ **Weak Example**:
"Attend trade shows in Europe to meet potential distributors and increase brand awareness in the region."

✅ **Strong Example**:
"Attend MedTech Innovation Summit in Frankfurt, Germany (May 14-16, 2027) to meet 15 pre-qualified German hospital procurement managers identified through B2B matchmaking service (Category G). These contacts represent €8.2M in annual medical device procurement across 3 major hospital networks in Bavaria and North Rhine-Westphalia. Trade show provides opportunity to demonstrate our surgical robotics platform to decision-makers and secure initial pilot project agreements. Expected outcome: 2-3 signed pilot agreements by Q3 2027."

**⚠️ Red Flags (Incomplete Information)**:
- Generic descriptions ("networking", "market exploration")
- No justification for cost estimates
- Activities marked "TBD" or "To Be Confirmed"
- Vague outcomes ("increase sales", "build awareness")
- No specific contacts, partners, or leads identified
- Budget estimates significantly above or below market rates without justification

### 4. Avoid Common Pitfalls

**Applications Are Weaker When They**:

**Don't Explain Export Growth Connection**:
- Activities described but not connected to export objectives
- No explanation of how activity leads to sales
- Missing the "so what?" - why this activity matters
- Confusing means with ends (attending trade show ≠ outcome; securing distribution agreement = outcome)

**Lack Coherent Strategy**:
- Targeting too many disparate markets without clear rationale
- Activities don't build on each other logically
- Mixing short-term and long-term objectives without prioritization
- No clear market entry sequence

**Give Insufficient Detail**:
- Vague activity descriptions
- No cost breakdown or justification
- Missing key information (dates, locations, participants)
- Unclear expected outcomes

**Propose Activities Without Planning**:
- No evidence of prior market research
- Haven't identified key contacts or partners
- No understanding of market requirements
- Requesting funding for activities that should be prerequisites (market research before deciding on trade shows)

**Rush Submission**:
- Obvious errors or inconsistencies
- Incomplete sections
- Missing key information
- Poor formatting making application hard to review

**Common Mistakes (2026-27 Specific)**:

❌ **Mixing U.S. with Other Markets**:
- Violates U.S. exclusivity rule
- **Automatic rejection**

❌ **Targeting Agriculture/Agri-Food**:
- No longer eligible sectors
- Should contact Agriculture Canada instead

❌ **Virtual Event Participation**:
- No longer eligible
- Must be in-person events only

❌ **Confusing Destination with Target Market**:
- Attending trade show in Germany ≠ targeting Germany
- Must clearly explain how destination supports target market

❌ **Ineligible Consultant Activities**:
- Requesting in-market representative services (not allowed)
- Consultant implementation work vs. advice only
- Monthly retainers for ongoing activities

❌ **Website Landing Pages**:
- No longer eligible (changed from 2025-26)
- Only website **translation** eligible

### 5. Use Evidence and Context

**Competitive Applications Supported By**:

**Market Research and Data**:
- Market size estimates with sources cited
- Growth rate projections from reputable sources
- Competitor analysis showing market gap/opportunity
- Regulatory/trade barrier research
- Customer needs assessment or validation

**Evidence of Capacity**:
- Team roles and relevant expertise
  - Who will execute activities?
  - What export/international experience do they have?
  - Are roles clearly defined?
- Previous export efforts or international experience
- Financial capacity to fund 50% cost-share
- Production capacity to fulfill international orders
- Quality certifications or compliance documentation

**Clear Value Proposition**:
- How funding reduces risk (e.g., testing market before full commitment)
- How funding improves export outcomes (e.g., accelerates market entry timeline)
- Why now is the right time (market window, competitive advantage, trade agreements)
- What company brings to target market (competitive advantages, unique value)

**Export Diversification Linkage**:
- Alignment with Canada's trade diversification goals
- Contribution to doubling non-U.S. exports
- How activities support Canada's trade strategy
- Potential for long-term export growth

**Types of Evidence to Include**:

**Market Opportunity**:
- Market size data (TAM, SAM, SOM)
- Growth rate projections
- Industry reports or studies
- Trade statistics
- Regulatory landscape analysis

**Customer Validation**:
- Letters of interest from potential buyers
- MOUs or preliminary agreements
- Pilot project commitments
- Customer research findings
- Trade show registration confirmations

**Company Capacity**:
- Team bios highlighting relevant experience
- Past export sales data (if any)
- Production capacity documentation
- Quality certifications (ISO, etc.)
- Financial statements showing stability

**Strategic Fit**:
- Export strategy documents
- Business plan excerpts
- Product-market fit analysis
- Competitive positioning research

**⚠️ Weak Evidence**:
- Claims without sources
- Outdated data (pre-2024)
- Generalized industry reports not specific to target market
- Assumptions presented as facts
- Circular reasoning ("market is attractive because we want to enter it")

### 6. Prepare Early and Review Carefully

**Because Program is Competitive**:

**Start Early**:
- Allow **2-4 weeks minimum** for quality application preparation
- Don't wait until last day of application window
- Build in time for client feedback and revisions
- Account for information gathering time

**Use Official Guidance**:
- Review CanExport SMEs Applicant's Guide 2026-27
- Check "What's New for 2026-27" page
- Review Tips for Submitting Strong Applications
- Consult Trade Commissioner Service resources
- Review eligible expense categories carefully

**Review All Sections Before Submission**:
- Ensure completeness (no TBDs or placeholders)
- Check for internal consistency
- Verify all activities link to target markets
- Confirm budget calculations are correct
- Validate that all requirements are met

**Ensure Alignment with 2026-27 Changes**:
- Not targeting agriculture/agri-food sectors
- Following U.S. exclusivity rule (U.S. OR other markets, not both)
- Only in-person trade events listed
- Per diem calculated at $600/day (not old $400 rate)
- Website adaptation = translation only
- Understanding updated consultant definitions

**Have Colleague Review**:
- Fresh eyes catch errors and gaps
- Test whether strategy is clear to someone unfamiliar with project
- Verify technical accuracy
- Check readability and flow
- Ensure persuasiveness

**Quality Markers of Well-Prepared Applications**:
- Align with program objectives and current-year priorities
- Demonstrate understanding of evaluation criteria
- Provide specific, evidence-based information
- Show coherent export development strategy
- Anticipate and address potential evaluator concerns
- Professional presentation and organization
- Complete and accurate information
- Clear narrative throughout

**⚠️ Signs of Rushed Applications**:
- Typos and formatting inconsistencies
- Generic descriptions (copy-paste from guidelines)
- Incomplete sections or placeholder text
- Budget doesn't align with activities described
- Inconsistent information across sections
- Missing required details
- No evidence or supporting documentation

## Assessment Criteria (What Evaluators Look For)

While official evaluation rubric is not public, competitive applications demonstrate:

**1. Export Readiness**:
- Company prepared to execute international activities
- Realistic understanding of market entry requirements
- Financial and operational capacity
- Previous export experience (if applicable)

**2. Market Opportunity**:
- Clear evidence of market potential
- Realistic assessment of market size and competition
- Understanding of market requirements and barriers
- Strategic fit between company offering and market needs

**3. Strategic Plan**:
- Logical sequence of activities
- Activities appropriate for market entry stage
- Clear connection between activities and outcomes
- Realistic timeline and resource allocation

**4. Incrementality**:
- Activities are **new** (not ongoing operations)
- Targeting **new** markets (meeting program definition)
- Activities specifically designed for international market entry
- Not using program to fund business-as-usual activities

**5. Alignment with Trade Diversification**:
- Priority for non-U.S. markets
- Contribution to Canada's trade diversification goals
- Long-term export potential
- Sustainable export development plan

## Grant Writer's Competitive Application Checklist

Before submitting application, verify:

**Eligibility & Compliance**:
- [ ] Company meets size requirements (3-500 employees, $300K-$100M revenue)
- [ ] Not targeting excluded sectors (agriculture, agri-food, fish/seafood)
- [ ] Following U.S. exclusivity rule (not mixing U.S. with other markets)
- [ ] All target markets are "new" per program definition
- [ ] All proposed activities are eligible under 2026-27 rules
- [ ] Only in-person trade events listed (no virtual)
- [ ] Consultant activities are advice only (not in-market representation)

**Strategic Quality**:
- [ ] Clear rationale for each target market selection
- [ ] All activities logically linked to specific target markets
- [ ] Coherent market entry strategy evident
- [ ] Activities build on each other sequentially
- [ ] Expected outcomes are specific and measurable
- [ ] Timeline is realistic and well-planned

**Detail & Evidence**:
- [ ] Activity descriptions are specific (who, what, when, where, why, expected outcome)
- [ ] Budget estimates are realistic and justified
- [ ] Market opportunity supported by data/research
- [ ] Company capacity clearly demonstrated
- [ ] All required information provided (no TBDs or placeholders)

**Presentation**:
- [ ] No typos or formatting errors
- [ ] Internally consistent information
- [ ] Clear and professional writing
- [ ] Easy for evaluator to understand strategy
- [ ] Persuasive narrative throughout

**Competitive Positioning**:
- [ ] Demonstrates strong export readiness
- [ ] Shows evidence-based market selection
- [ ] Highlights competitive advantages
- [ ] Addresses potential concerns proactively
- [ ] Aligns with trade diversification priority

## Grant Writer Usage Notes

Use this skill when:
- Reviewing client applications before submission
- Advising clients on improving application competitiveness
- Identifying weaknesses in proposed strategies
- Coaching clients on what evaluators look for
- Conducting readiness assessments

**Key Message to Clients**:
Meeting eligibility ≠ guaranteed funding. Applications are assessed competitively. The stronger your application demonstrates readiness, strategy, and alignment with program objectives, the better your approval odds.

**Strategic Positioning Advice**:
- Non-U.S. markets have 90% of funding pool AND strategic priority = better odds
- U.S. projects face longer processing, higher competition, smaller funding pool
- Defence sector applications receive priority processing
- Evidence-based applications with specific plans outperform generic applications
- Early preparation allows time for quality development

**Common Revision Recommendations**:
1. Add specificity (names, dates, numbers, sources)
2. Strengthen market linkage (explain how destination supports target market)
3. Provide evidence (data, research, validation)
4. Clarify expected outcomes (quantify results)
5. Tighten strategic narrative (connect all activities to coherent plan)
6. Remove/replace ineligible activities
7. Justify cost estimates
8. Demonstrate company readiness/capacity
