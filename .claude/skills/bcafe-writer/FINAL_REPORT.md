# BCAFE Final Progress Report Writing Guide

**Purpose**: Guide users through completing the mandatory BCAFE Final Progress Report template with comprehensive, compliant responses

---

## Overview

The **Final Progress Report** must be submitted to admin@bcagricultureandfoodexportprogram.ca **on or before the due date specified in Schedule 'A' of your Funding Agreement** (typically within 90 days of project completion).

**Required Submissions**:
1. **Final Progress Report** (this template)
2. **Expense Report** (separate template provided by program)
3. **Supporting Documentation** (photos, videos, receipts per section requirements)

---

## Section 1.0: PROJECT IDENTIFICATION

### Required Information

**Fields to Complete**:
- Project Identification (number from funding agreement)
- Legal Company Name (exactly as registered)
- Project Name (from approved application)
- Application Identifier (from https://mnp-apps.powerappsportals.com/)
- Project Start Date (from Schedule A - typically April 1, 2026)
- Project End Date (from Schedule A - typically March 1, 2027)
- Prepared By (name and title)
- Date Submitted

### Guidance

✅ **Use exact dates and names from your Funding Agreement Schedule A**
✅ **Application Identifier**: Log into the MNP portal and retrieve your application ID
✅ **Legal Company Name**: Must match BC business registration exactly

## Section 1.1: PROJECT OVERVIEW (MANDATORY)

### Project Summary Overview

**What This Section Requires**:
1. **Detailed summary of the project**
2. **Markets targeted** (country AND region, specify new vs. existing)
3. **Barriers to implementation encountered**
4. **Lessons learned** for future program participants
5. **Reference to eligible activity outputs** from Schedule A

### Writing Approach

**Structure Your Response** (aim for 500-800 words):

**Paragraph 1 - Project Overview & Markets**:
- Restate project objective and target markets
- Specify NEW vs EXISTING markets (critical distinction)
- Identify specific regions within countries (e.g., "California, USA" not just "USA")

**Paragraph 2 - Activities Completed**:
- List major activities completed (reference Schedule A outputs)
- Connect activities to original application plan
- Note any completed outputs from approved budget

**Paragraph 3 - Barriers Encountered**:
- Be honest about challenges (supply chain, timelines, market conditions, regulatory)
- Explain how barriers were addressed or mitigated
- Show problem-solving and adaptability

**Paragraph 4 - Lessons Learned**:
- Specific, actionable insights for future participants
- What you'd do differently next time
- Advice for similar organizations entering same markets

### Example Response Structure

```
[Organization Name] completed its BCAFE-funded export market development project
targeting [New Market: Country, Region] between [Start Date] and [End Date]. The
project focused on establishing market presence through [summary of main activities
from Schedule A]. [Market Name] represents a new export market for our organization,
with zero previous sales and no existing distribution channels prior to this project.

Throughout the project period, we successfully completed the following approved
activity outputs as outlined in Schedule A:
• Export-focused marketing collateral: [Specific outputs - e.g., translated product
  catalogs, market-specific packaging designs, social media ad campaigns]
• Trade show participation: [Specific events - e.g., Natural Products Expo West
  (March 2027), with booth, product displays, and Buy BC branding]
• Consumer-focused promotional activities: [Specific activities - e.g., in-store
  tastings at 3 retail locations in California]

Key barriers encountered included [specific challenge 1, e.g., "unexpected delays
in obtaining USDA organic certification"], which resulted in [impact]. We addressed
this by [solution]. Additionally, [specific challenge 2, e.g., "higher-than-expected
shipping costs to target market"] required [adjustment to strategy or budget].

Lessons learned that may benefit future program participants:
1. [Specific lesson - e.g., "Allow 4-6 months for regulatory/certification processes
   in US market, not the 2-3 months initially estimated"]
2. [Specific lesson - e.g., "Trade show success requires 3-4 months of pre-event
   outreach to buyers; last-minute booking yielded fewer qualified leads"]
3. [Specific lesson - e.g., "Working with in-market broker/agent essential for
   navigating retail buyer relationships in [Market]"]

The project achieved [key outcome metric, e.g., "$X in confirmed export sales,
Y qualified partnerships, Z retail placements"], establishing a foundation for
sustained export growth in [Market].
```

## Common Final Report Mistakes to Avoid

### ❌ Mistake 1: Vague Project Summary
**Problem**: "We had a successful project and entered new markets."
**Fix**: "We successfully entered the United States market (California region) - a NEW market with zero previous sales - achieving $47,000 in confirmed export sales through 3 distributor partnerships and 12 retail placements."

### ❌ Mistake 2: Missing Market Specificity (NEW vs EXISTING)
**Problem**: "We targeted the United States and Japan."
**Fix**: "We targeted California, USA (NEW market - no previous sales) and Tokyo region, Japan (EXISTING market - $12K previous sales in 2025, expanding to new distribution channels)."

### ❌ Mistake 3: Not Referencing Schedule A Outputs
**Problem**: "We created marketing materials and attended a trade show."
**Fix**: "We completed the following approved activity outputs as outlined in Schedule A: (1) Product catalog translation (Japanese), (2) Social media ad campaign (Instagram/Facebook), (3) Natural Products Expo West trade show participation."

### ❌ Mistake 4: Generic Lessons Learned
**Problem**: "Export markets are challenging and require preparation."
**Fix**: "Allow minimum 4-6 months for USDA organic certification when targeting US market (we initially budgeted 2 months, causing 4-month delay). Engage certification consultant early rather than attempting DIY approach."

### ❌ Mistake 5: Incomplete Activity Documentation
**Problem**: Listing activities but not providing required photos/videos
**Fix**: Ensure EVERY trade show, consumer promotion, and marketing campaign has corresponding high-resolution documentation uploaded with clear Buy BC branding visible

### ❌ Mistake 6: No Explanation for Budget Variances
**Problem**: Actual spend differs 25% from approved without explanation
**Fix**: Proactively explain ANY variance >10%, document reallocation decisions, confirm total budget and cash match maintained

### ❌ Mistake 7: Missing Documentation Links
**Problem**: Stating "photos attached" but forgetting to include Dropbox link or attach files
**Fix**: Double-check Dropbox/OneDrive link is included in template AND test link accessibility before submission

### ❌ Mistake 8: Unclear Expense Descriptions in Financial Reporting

## Tips for Strong Final Reports

### ✅ Tip 1: Be Specific and Quantify Everything
Every claim should have a number: leads generated, sales achieved, people reached, materials distributed, meetings held, partnerships formed.

### ✅ Tip 2: Connect Activities to Outcomes
Don't just list what you did - explain what resulted from each activity. "Trade show participation → 127 leads → 18 sample requests → 5 proposals → 2 signed distribution agreements"

### ✅ Tip 3: Tell the Story of Market Entry Journey
Take the reviewer through your market entry progression: research → preparation → market testing → relationship building → sales → sustainable presence

### ✅ Tip 4: Be Honest About Challenges
Program management learns from participant experiences. Honest reporting about barriers helps improve future program design and helps other participants anticipate challenges.

### ✅ Tip 5: Showcase Buy BC Branding Prominently
Make it easy for reviewers to see Buy BC compliance - include multiple clear photos showing branding on booth materials, promotional items, marketing collateral.

### ✅ Tip 6: Organize Documentation Meticulously
Well-organized photo folders with clear file names (e.g., "Booth-Setup-Wide-Angle-Buy-BC-Branding.jpg") demonstrate professionalism and make review process smooth.

### ✅ Tip 7: Proofread for Compliance
Cross-reference every activity, expense, and outcome against your original Funding Agreement Schedule A to ensure alignment and note any changes.

### ✅ Tip 8: Submit Early
Don't wait until deadline day. Submit 1-2 weeks early to allow time to address any questions or requests for clarification from program management.
