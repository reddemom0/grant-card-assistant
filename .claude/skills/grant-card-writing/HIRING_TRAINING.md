# Grant Card Writing — Hiring & Training

Format spec for **Hiring & Training** grants. Load this sub-skill after detecting that the program funds wage subsidies, student placements, training programs, apprenticeships, or workforce development.

This sub-skill assumes the agent has already loaded the parent `OVERVIEW` skill and applied its general rules. Everything here is type-specific.

---

## Three Sub-Types

This grant type covers three adjacent sub-types. Detect which one applies and reflect it in the Preview and Grant Overview:

1. **Pure Hiring Grants** — wage subsidies for net-new hires
2. **Pure Training Grants** — upskilling/retraining current or future employees
3. **Hybrid Hiring + Training Grants** — wage subsidy + onboarding/training obligations

The eligibility sub-sections in Section 4 vary depending on which sub-type applies.

---

## What Hiring & Training Grants Consistently Emphasize

- **Wage subsidy** — most hiring programs offer a percentage of wages for a defined period
- **Equity groups** — programs often target hires from underrepresented categories (women, BIPOC, persons with disabilities, newcomers)
- **Youth hiring** — many programs specifically target hires under age 30
- **Training activities** — courses, formats, eligible providers, durations

These themes should appear naturally across the Preview, Grant Overview, and Eligibility sections.

## Section 2: Preview Description

**Aim:** A quick, high-level explanation of what the program does and why it exists. Help someone instantly decide: "Is this relevant to me?"

Quickly explain:
- who the employer can hire/train
- what cost is being offset
- why the program exists

**Length & depth:**
- 1–2 sentences only
- Paragraph format, not bullets
- No dollar amounts, no eligibility details, no process

**Language style:**
- Plain business language
- Outcome-focused (what the business gets)
- No jargon, no program mechanics

**Formatting:**
- Single paragraph
- No bullets

**AI Rule:** Clearly identify whether the funding supports hiring, training, or both.

**Examples by sub-type:**

*Hiring:* "This program helps Nova Scotia businesses offset the cost of hiring recent master's and PhD graduates into innovation-focused roles."

*Training:* "This program helps employers reduce employee training costs by subsidizing external workforce training."

*Hybrid:* "This program helps employers hire youth while reducing both wage and onboarding costs."

## Section 3: General Requirements

### 3.1 Grant Overview

**Aim:** Add key qualifiers to the preview — funding structure, program scope, and who it's for.

**Length & depth:**
- 2–4 sentences max
- Includes:
  - funding model (%, max, in-kind)
  - key eligibility signal
  - program intent

**Language style:**
- Still plain, but slightly more detailed
- Informational, not instructional

**Formatting:**
- Paragraph format
- Followed by "Grant Value" as a separate line or block

**AI Rule:** Expand the preview by adding who it's for + how funding works, without going into lists.

### 3.2 Grant Value

**Aim:** Clearly quantify the financial or in-kind support.

**Length & depth:**
- As short as possible
- Include:
  - % of funding
  - maximum amounts
  - special tiers, funding range based on hours worked (if applicable)

**Language style:**
- Numeric and precise
- No explanation unless necessary

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points or short lines
- Separate streams/tiers clearly

**AI Rule:** Break funding into clean tiers when necessary.

### 3.3 Turnaround Time

**Aim:** Set expectations on how long approval takes.

**Length & depth:**
- One short line

**Language style:**
- Direct and factual

**Formatting:**
- "Within X weeks/months" format

**AI Rule:** Never explain, just state timing. If intake deadlines matter more than approval timing, list deadlines.

## Section 4: Eligibility Criteria

Hiring & Training has a different eligibility structure than other grant types because **employer eligibility and candidate eligibility are distinct**. Include each sub-section only if clearly cited.

### 4.1 Eligible Applicants (Employers)

**Aim:** Define who qualifies at a business level.

**Length & depth:**
- 4–8 bullet points
- Include:
  - business type
  - location
  - business registration
  - operational history
  - sector restrictions
  - employee size restrictions
  - industry or strategic focus

**Language style:**
- Clear, eligibility-driven
- No explanation or examples unless necessary
- Include additional clarifications as a "Note" bullet in *italics*

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points ending with semicolons
- Group logically (core eligibility → additional criteria → priorities)

**AI Rule:** Focus strictly on employer eligibility.

### 4.2 Ineligible Applicants (Employers)

Same aim, structure, and formatting as Eligible Applicants. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.3 Eligible Participants (Candidates/Trainees)

**Aim:** Define who can be hired or trained under this program.

**Length & depth:**
- Bullet list
- Moderate length (not exhaustive unless necessary)
- Include additional clarifications as a "Note" bullet in *italics*

**Language style:**
- Functional

**Common criteria:**
- age
- education
- residency
- citizenship
- employment status
- underrepresented groups

**Observed pattern:**
- This section is often more complex than employer eligibility.
- Examples: graduate recency requirements, youth age bands, unemployment requirements, residency requirements

**AI Rule:** Keep candidate eligibility completely separate from employer eligibility.

### 4.4 Eligible Placements

**Aim:** Define job requirements (for Hiring) or training requirements (for Training).

**For Hiring programs**, include:
- full-time / part-time
- permanent / temporary
- wage thresholds
- net-new jobs
- eligible duties

**For Training programs**, include:
- eligible courses
- training formats
- eligible training providers
- duration

**For Hybrid programs (Hiring + Training)**, include:
- placement duration
- mentorship obligations
- training requirements

**Length & depth:**
- Bullet list
- Moderate length (not exhaustive unless necessary)
- Include additional clarifications as a "Note" bullet in *italics*

**Language style:**
- Functional

**AI Rule:** This section defines what the funded workforce activity must look like.

### 4.5 Eligible / Ineligible Expenses

**Aim:** Define what costs are covered.

**Length & depth:**
- Bullet points

**Language style:**
- Specific and practical

**Formatting:**
- Include examples where helpful
- Keep long exclusions grouped logically

**Hiring programs usually cover:**
- wages
- payroll costs
- direct employment costs

**Training programs usually cover:**
- tuition
- materials
- travel
- exams

**AI Rule:** Only include if clearly defined. Much more important in training grants than hiring grants.

## Section 5: Program Details

**Aim:** Explain how the program actually runs. Includes:
- hiring deadlines
- placement duration
- reporting requirements
- milestone payments
- reimbursement schedule
- claim requirements
- any constraints (minimum hours thresholds, employment retention obligations, etc.)

**Length & depth:**
- 5–15 concise bullet points

**Language style:**
- Operational, structured

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points ending with semicolons
- Group by:
  - structure (phases, streams)
  - timelines
  - limits (applications, duration)

**AI Rule:** This is the "how it works" section, not eligibility or funding. It should explain operational mechanics after approval.

## Section 7: Application Process

### 7.1 Condensed Grant Steps

1–5 steps in plain business language. Example:

1. Apply;
2. (Optional) Additional steps;
3. Approval;
4. Completion;
5. Reimbursement.

**AI Rule:** Keep generic unless program-specific steps are clearly defined.

### 7.2 Extended Grant Steps

Same 1–5 steps with detail. Keep concise; use bullet points.

For each step, cover relevant items from:
- Pre-application prep work (budget, assessment, pre-eligibility survey)
- Timing context (Step 1 approval within X weeks, then Step 2)
- Documentation and information required
- Obtaining documents like quotes or letters of support
- Post-approval claims and reporting process and frequency

## Type-Specific Reminders

- **Detect the sub-type early.** Pure Hiring, Pure Training, or Hybrid? Surface this in Preview and Grant Overview.
- **Employer vs. candidate eligibility are separate.** Section 4.1 covers the business; Section 4.3 covers the person being hired or trained. Never blend them.
- **Equity group targeting.** Many programs include enhanced funding rates for hires from underrepresented groups. Surface this in Grant Value when cited.
- **Youth programs.** Many hiring programs target candidates under age 30 (or 29). Make age bands prominent in Eligible Participants when cited.
- **Net-new hires.** Pure Hiring programs almost always require the placement to be "net new" — beyond the employer's baseline. Surface this in Eligible Placements.
