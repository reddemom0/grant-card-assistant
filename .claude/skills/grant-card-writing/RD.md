# Grant Card Writing — R&D

Format spec for **Research & Development** grants. Load this sub-skill after detecting that the program funds research, prototyping, validation, or commercialization of technical innovation.

This sub-skill assumes the agent has already loaded the parent `OVERVIEW` skill and applied its general rules. Everything here is R&D-specific.

---

## What R&D Grants Consistently Emphasize

When writing an R&D card, anchor the content around these themes:

- **TRL levels** (Technology Readiness Levels)
- **Scalability**
- **Validation** (lab + real-world)
- **Quantifiable outcomes** — GHG reduction, efficiency gains, performance improvements
- **Clear benefit** — economic, environmental, intellectual property

These themes should appear naturally across the Preview, Grant Overview, Eligible Projects, and Eligible Activities sections.

## Section 2: Preview Description

**Aim:** Explain what kind of innovation the program supports and what problem it is trying to solve.

**Length & depth:**
- 2–3 lines maximum (1–2 sentences)
- Paragraph format, not bullets
- No dollar amounts, no percentages, no eligibility details, no deadlines, no process, no project examples, no program history, no government commitments

**Language style:**
- Plain business language ("Layman's language")
- Outcome + innovation focused
- Often includes sector/problem (e.g., emissions, energy, AI)
- No jargon, no program mechanics

**Formatting:**
- Single short paragraph
- No bullets

**AI Rule:** Describe innovation type + impact outcome. Not funding, not process. The Preview answers "what is this program?" — nothing else. If a sentence contains a number, a percentage, a date, or the word "eligible," remove it.

## Section 3: General Requirements

This section has three sub-blocks: Grant Overview, Grant Value, Turnaround Time.

### 3.1 Grant Overview

**Aim:** Expand the Preview by adding the key details an applicant needs to decide if the program is a fit for them. Answers: "What is this program, and is it a fit for me?" For R&D programs, anchor the overview in funding scale + innovation stage + commercialization intent.

**Length & depth:**
- 3–4 lines maximum (2–4 sentences)
- Must include:
  - funding range or typical size (per-applicant value, not total program budget)
  - stage of development (TRL or equivalent)
  - commercialization or validation goal
- Add additional context only when relevant: contribution percentage, expected outcomes, who it's for

**Language style:**
- Still plain, but more technical than other grant types (TRL, prototype, scale-up are acceptable)
- Informational, not instructional
- Not narrative history — do not describe when the program launched or government priorities

**Formatting:**
- Paragraph format
- Followed by "Grant Value" as a separate line or block

**Critical distinction — total program budget vs. grant value:**
- The program's total budget (e.g., "$291 million initiative") is the funding allocated to run the overall program. Rarely useful to an individual applicant.
- The grant value is what an individual applicant can receive.
- Always report grant value to the applicant. Never use total program budget as the headline figure.

**AI Rule:** Anchor the program in stage of innovation + funding scale + commercialization intent. If a sentence describes program mechanics, history, or government goals rather than what's offered to the applicant, remove it.

### 3.2 Grant Value

**Aim:** Clearly quantify the financial or in-kind support. Distinguish averages vs. caps vs. cost-share.

**Length & depth:**
- As short as possible
- Bullets in this order:
  - % contribution (coverage percentage of eligible costs)
  - maximum funding amount per applicant
  - average funding (if relevant — add a NOTE if averages differ from caps)
  - additional funding for specific project types or applicant groups (if applicable)
  - applicant contribution requirement (if applicable)

**Language style:**
- Numeric and precise
- No explanation unless necessary

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points or short lines
- Use consistent wording across cards: "Up to X% of eligible costs," "Up to $X per [project/applicant/year]"

**Critical distinction — program budget vs. applicant value:**
- Report grant value to the applicant, not the program's total budget.
- If the source material only gives a total program budget without per-applicant figures, state that the per-applicant maximum is not specified in source material. Do not divide, estimate, or infer.
- If a value isn't in the source material, omit the bullet. Do not fabricate.

**AI Rule:** Always distinguish between maximum funding and realistic/average funding.

### 3.3 Turnaround Time

**Aim:** Set expectations on how long approval takes.

**Length & depth:**
- One short line

**Language style:**
- Direct and factual
- Can include milestone dates (EOI, proposal, decision)

**Formatting:**
- "Within X weeks/months" format

**AI Rule:** Include timeline context if multi-stage — not just a duration.

## Section 4: Eligibility Criteria

Six sub-sections. Include each only if clearly cited in program guidelines or the program webpage. Never infer ineligibility.

### 4.1 Eligible Applicants

**Aim:** Define who can lead and deliver a technical project.

**Length & depth:**
- 4–8 bullet points
- Include:
  - business type
  - location
  - size/revenue
  - sector or strategic focus

**Language style:**
- Clear, eligibility-driven
- Capability-focused
- No explanation or examples unless necessary
- Include additional clarifications as a "Note" bullet under Eligible Applicants

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points ending with semicolons
- Group logically (core eligibility → additional criteria → priorities)

**AI Rule:** Only include decision-making criteria, not descriptive context. Go beyond "who they are" — include their ability to deliver the project and fit for the program mandate.

### 4.2 Ineligible Applicants

Same aim, structure, and formatting as Eligible Applicants. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.3 Eligible Projects

**Aim:** Define what a fundable project looks like.

**Length & depth:**
- 5–10 bullets

**Language style:**
- Technical but clear

**Formatting:**
- Start with high-level criteria, then examples
- Must show:
  - innovation
  - measurable outcomes
  - real-world validation
  - scalability

**AI Rule:** This section defines what gets funded, not just activities.

### 4.4 Ineligible Projects

Same structure, language, and formatting as Eligible Projects. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.5 Eligible Activities

**Aim:** Describe the work involved in advancing the technology.

**Length & depth:**
- 5–8 bullets
- Moderate length (not exhaustive unless necessary)
- Include additional clarifications as a "Note" bullet under Eligible Activities

**Language style:**
- Functional verbs: developing, assessing, implementing
- Activities consistently include:
  - prototyping
  - testing (lab + real-world)
  - integration
  - demonstration
  - scaling

**Formatting:**
- Group by theme if complex

**AI Rule:** Focus on technical progression steps, not planning or advisory.

### 4.6 Ineligible Activities

Same aim, structure, and formatting as Eligible Activities. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.7 Eligible Expenses

**Aim:** Define what costs are covered.

**Length & depth:**
- Bullet points
- More detailed than activities

**Language style:**
- Specific and practical

**Formatting:**
- Include examples where helpful

**Observed pattern for R&D:**
- Strong emphasis on:
  - excluding operational costs
  - excluding capital purchases
  - limiting scope to advisory/planning

**AI Rule:** Translate policy/program language into clear business terms.

### 4.8 Ineligible Expenses

Define what costs are excluded. Same structure and formatting as Eligible Expenses. Include only if clearly cited.

## Section 5: Program Details

**Aim:** Explain how the funding program operates structurally. Includes:
- constraints (1 application, ability to submit multiple projects, deadlines)
- multi-stage process (EOI → Proposal → Decision)
- milestone-based payments
- project timelines (start, duration)
- evaluation criteria

**Length & depth:**
- 6–15 concise bullet points

**Language style:**
- Operational, structured
- Vary sentence structure across bullets. Avoid stacking many short, formulaic openings ("Projects must...", "Funding is...", "Applicants must..."). Combine related rules into flowing phrasing where natural. Write like a knowledgeable consultant briefing a client, not a policy document.

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points ending with semicolons
- Group by:
  - structure (phases, streams)
  - timelines
  - limits (applications, duration)
  - evaluation criteria

**AI Rule:** This section explains program mechanics, not eligibility.

## Section 6: Best Practices and Forms

Three sub-blocks: Eligible Best Practices, Process Best Practices, Forms.

### 6.1 Eligible Best Practices

**Aim:** Help applicants submit a stronger technical project.

**Length & depth:**
- 2–5 bullets

**Language style:**
- Advisory tone

**Typical content:**
- eligibility nuances
- what makes a competitive R&D project (TRL clarity, validation plan, quantifiable outcomes)

**AI Rule:** Focus on eligibility and project-strength factors specific to R&D evaluation.

### 6.2 Process Best Practices

**Aim:** Help applicants navigate the process more effectively.

**Length & depth:**
- 2–5 bullets

**Language style:**
- Practical and strategic

**Typical content:**
- preparation tips
- any online portal logins that need to be created
- application strategy (EOI before full proposal, etc.)
- supplementary documentation required

**AI Rule:** Focus on process navigation — what to do and in what order.

### 6.3 Forms

Direct access to application materials. No descriptions, just links.

- Program Website: [URL from source material]
- Program Guidelines: [URL from source material]
- Application Portal: [URL from source material]

**Rules:**
- Use these three labels exactly (Title Case). Do not invent alternative labels (e.g., "FCM Portal," "Eligible Costs Document," "FAQ").
- If a URL is in the source material, include it as a clickable link.
- If a URL is not in the source material, omit that bullet entirely. Do not write "[click here]," "TBD," or any placeholder.
- Do not add new rows beyond these three labels, even if the source material contains additional resources.

**AI Rule:** Exactly three possible rows. Include each only if you have a real URL from the source. No descriptions, no placeholders, no extras.

## Section 7: Application Process

Two sub-blocks: Condensed Grant Steps, Extended Grant Steps.

### 7.1 Condensed Grant Steps

1 to 5 steps in plain business language without much detail. Example:

1. Confirm eligibility and submit Expression of Interest;
2. If shortlisted, prepare full proposal with technical and budget details;
3. Submit full proposal by deadline;
4. Project review and decision;
5. Sign agreement and begin project work with milestone-based payments.

**AI Rule:** For R&D, often a multi-stage process (EOI → Proposal → Decision). Reflect this in the steps.

### 7.2 Extended Grant Steps

Same 1–5 steps but with important details under each step. Keep concise; use bullet points whenever possible.

For each step, cover relevant items from:
- Pre-application prep work (i.e. budget, TRL assessment, pre-eligibility survey)
- Timing context (i.e. Step 1 EOI decision within 4 weeks, then prepare Step 2)
- Documentation and information required
- Obtaining documents like quotes, letters of support, or partner agreements
- Post-approval claims and reporting process and frequency

## R&D-Specific Reminders

- **TRL is a core concept.** Most R&D programs target a specific TRL range (e.g., TRL 4–7). Surface this in Grant Overview or Eligible Projects.
- **Validation matters.** Lab validation alone is rarely enough — real-world or industrial validation is often required. Reflect this in Eligible Activities and Best Practices.
- **Quantifiable outcomes.** R&D evaluators want measurable results (GHG reduced, efficiency gained, IP filed). Surface this when the program emphasizes it.
- **Commercialization intent.** Pure research is rarely funded. Programs almost always expect a path to commercial application. Anchor the Grant Overview around this.
