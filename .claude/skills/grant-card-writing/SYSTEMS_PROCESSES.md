# Grant Card Writing — Systems & Processes

Format spec for **Systems & Processes** grants. Load this sub-skill after detecting that the program funds software adoption, AI tools, digitalization, process improvement, or automation.

This sub-skill assumes the agent has already loaded the parent `OVERVIEW` skill and applied its general rules. Everything here is type-specific.

---

## What Systems & Processes Grants Consistently Emphasize

- **Operational system improvement** — the way a business operates internally
- **Process efficiency** — streamlining, automation, digitalization
- **Technology adoption** — software, AI, energy-efficient systems
- **Stream-based structures** — programs often have multiple streams with different funding caps

These themes should appear naturally across the Preview, Grant Overview, and Eligible Projects sections.

## Section 2: Preview Description

**Aim:** Explain what system, process, or operational challenge the program supports.

**Length & depth:**
- 1–2 sentences only
- Paragraph format, not bullets
- No dollar amounts, no eligibility details, no process

**Language style:**
- Plain business language
- Outcome-focused (what the business gets)
- No jargon, no program mechanics

**Formatting:**
- Single paragraph
- No bullets

**AI Rule:** Describe what operational system is being improved + the intended business or environmental outcome.

## Section 3: General Requirements

### 3.1 Grant Overview

**Aim:** Add key qualifiers to the preview — funding structure, program scope, and who it's for.

**Length & depth:**
- 2–4 sentences max
- Includes:
  - funding model (%, max $, in-kind contribution)
  - key eligibility signal
  - program intent

**Language style:**
- Still plain, but slightly more detailed
- Informational, not instructional

**Formatting:**
- Paragraph format
- Followed by "Grant Value" as a separate line or block

**AI Rule:** Expand the preview by adding who it's for, how much funding is provided, and how funding works — without going into lists.

### 3.2 Grant Value

**Aim:** Clearly quantify the financial or in-kind support.

**Length & depth:**
- As short as possible
- Include:
  - % of funding
  - maximum amounts
  - percentage covered
  - stream-specific limits
  - special caps or combined limits

**Language style:**
- Numeric and precise
- No explanation unless necessary

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points or short lines
- Separate streams/tiers clearly

**AI Rule:** Use bullets when there are multiple streams, caps, or cost-share rates.

### 3.3 Turnaround Time

**Aim:** Set expectations on how long approval takes.

**Length & depth:**
- One short line

**Language style:**
- Direct and factual

**Formatting:**
- "Within X weeks/months" format

**AI Rule:** Never explain, just state timing.

## Section 4: Eligibility Criteria

Include each sub-section only if clearly cited in program guidelines or webpage. Never infer ineligibility.

### 4.1 Eligible Applicants

**Aim:** Define who qualifies at a business level.

**Length & depth:**
- 4–8 bullet points
- Include:
  - business type
  - location
  - business registration
  - operational history
  - sector restrictions
  - employee size restrictions
  - industry or strategic focus
  - required plans or certifications

**Language style:**
- Clear, eligibility-driven
- No explanation or examples unless necessary
- Include additional clarifications as a "Note" bullet in *italics*

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points ending with semicolons
- Group logically (core eligibility → additional criteria → priorities)

**AI Rule:** Only include criteria about the applicant, not the project.

### 4.2 Ineligible Applicants

Same aim, structure, and formatting as Eligible Applicants. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.3 Eligible Projects

**Aim:** Define what types of operational improvements qualify.

**Length & depth:**
- 5–10 bullets
- Often grouped by stream

**Language style:**
- Technical but clear

**Formatting:**
- Start with high-level criteria, then examples

**Common project types:**
- equipment upgrades
- system installations
- process improvements
- energy efficiency upgrades
- commercialization or scale-up process changes

**AI Rule:** This section should answer: *"What kind of system/process improvement can be funded?"*

### 4.4 Ineligible Projects

Same structure, language, and formatting as Eligible Projects. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.5 Eligible Activities

**Aim:** Describe the specific actions the applicant may carry out.

**Length & depth:**
- 5–8 bullets
- Moderate length (not exhaustive unless necessary)
- Include additional clarifications as a "Note" bullet in *italics*

**Language style:**
- Functional verbs: developing, assessing, implementing
- Activities consistently include:
  - installing energy-efficient systems
  - replacing fossil-fuel equipment
  - implementing AI or technology
  - completing system upgrades

**Formatting:**
- Group by theme if complex

**AI Rule:** Activities describe what the applicant *does*. Projects describe what is being *funded overall*. Keep them separate.

### 4.6 Ineligible Activities

Same aim, structure, and formatting as Eligible Activities. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.7 Eligible Expenses

**Aim:** Define what costs are covered.

**Length & depth:**
- Bullet points
- More detailed than activities

**Language style:**
- Specific and practical

**Formatting:**
- Include examples where helpful

**AI Rule:** Translate policy/program language into clear business terms.

### 4.8 Ineligible Expenses

Same structure and formatting as Eligible Expenses. Include only if clearly cited.

## Section 5: Program Details

**Aim:** Explain how the program actually runs. Includes:
- project location requirements
- stream structure
- cost-share rules
- reimbursement basis
- project completion deadlines
- claim/payment rules
- approvals required before starting
- application limits
- evaluation priorities
- evaluation criteria (only when clearly stated)
- any other constraints

**Length & depth:**
- 5–15 concise bullet points

**Language style:**
- Operational, structured

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points ending with semicolons
- Group by:
  - structure (phases, streams)
  - timelines
  - limits (applications, duration)

**AI Rule:** This is the operational mechanics section. It explains how the grant works after eligibility is established — not eligibility or funding.

## Section 7: Application Process

### 7.1 Condensed Grant Steps

1–5 steps in plain business language. Typical flow:

1. Confirm eligibility and project stream;
2. Prepare project plan, quotes, budget, and required technical documents;
3. Submit application;
4. Receive approval before starting work;
5. Complete project and submit claim documents for reimbursement.

**AI Rule:** Keep generic unless program-specific steps are clearly defined.

### 7.2 Extended Grant Steps

Same 1–5 steps with detail. Keep concise; use bullet points.

For each step, cover relevant items from:
- Pre-application prep work (budget, assessment, pre-eligibility survey)
- Timing context (Step 1 approval within X weeks, then Step 2)
- Documentation and information required
- Obtaining documents like quotes or letters of support
- Post-approval claims and reporting process and frequency

## Type-Specific Reminders

- **Approval before starting.** Most Systems & Processes programs require formal approval before the applicant begins work. Surface this in Program Details and Application Process.
- **Stream structure.** Programs in this category frequently have multiple streams (e.g., software vs. equipment vs. process improvement) with different funding caps. Reflect the stream structure in Grant Value, Eligible Projects, and Program Details.
- **Projects vs. activities are distinct.** Eligible Projects = the type of improvement being funded. Eligible Activities = what the applicant does to deliver it. Never blur the line.
- **Reimbursement-based payment.** Most programs reimburse after project completion. Surface this in Program Details.
