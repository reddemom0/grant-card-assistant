# Grant Card Writing — Prizes, Awards & Contests

Format spec for **Prizes, Awards & Contests** programs. Load this sub-skill after detecting that the program is a competition where applicants win cash prizes, in-kind services, or recognition.

This sub-skill assumes the agent has already loaded the parent `OVERVIEW` skill and applied its general rules. Everything here is type-specific.

---

## Modified Structure for This Grant Type

Unlike other grant types, Prizes & Contests cards **do not** include:
- Eligible / Ineligible Projects
- Eligible / Ineligible Activities
- Eligible / Ineligible Expenses

The eligibility section only covers Eligible / Ineligible Applicants. Everything else lives in Program Details (competition mechanics) or Best Practices (entry strategy).

The reason: competitions don't fund *projects* — they recognize achievement or business profile. The card structure reflects that.

---

## What Prizes & Contests Consistently Emphasize

- **Recognition and prize structure** — cash, in-kind services, mentorship, exposure
- **Competition stages** — application → shortlist → pitch → winner selection
- **Judging criteria** — what makes a strong entry
- **Application deadlines** — usually a single hard deadline, not rolling intake
- **Prize tiers** — finalists, runners-up, grand prize winners

These themes should appear naturally across the Preview, Program Overview, and Prize Value sections.

## Section 2: Preview Description

**Aim:** A quick, high-level explanation of who the competition is for, what they can win, and what achievement or business profile is being recognized.

**Length & depth:**
- 1–2 sentences only
- Paragraph format, not bullets
- No dollar amounts, no eligibility details, no process

**Language style:**
- Plain business language
- Outcome-focused (what the business gets)
- No jargon, no program mechanics

**Formatting:**
- Single paragraph
- No bullets

**AI Rule:** Describe who can compete + prize/recognition type + competition purpose.

---

## Section 3: General Requirements

### 3.1 Grant Overview

**Aim:** Add key qualifiers to the preview — funding structure, program scope, and who it's for.

**Length & depth:**
- 2–4 sentences max
- Includes:
  - funding model (%, max, in-kind)
  - key eligibility signal
  - program intent

**Language style:**
- Still plain, but slightly more detailed
- Informational, not instructional

**Formatting:**
- Paragraph format
- Followed by "Prize Value" as a separate line or block

**AI Rule:** Expand the preview by adding who it's for + how funding works, without going into lists.

### 3.2 Prize Value

**Aim:** Clearly quantify the financial or in-kind support.

**Length & depth:**
- As short as possible
- Include:
  - % of funding
  - maximum amounts
  - special tiers, prize value range, etc.

**Language style:**
- Numeric and precise
- No explanation unless necessary

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points or short lines
- Separate prize tiers when applicable

**Typical content:**
- cash prize amounts
- in-kind services
- finalist awards
- annual / monthly / quarterly prize structure
- coaching, mentorship, exposure, or recognition benefits

**AI Rule:** Use **Prize Value** or **Award Value**, not Grant Value. Clearly list prize amounts and categories.

### 3.3 Turnaround Time

**Aim:** Show when finalists or winners are announced.

**Length & depth:**
- One short line

**Language style:**
- Direct and factual

**Formatting:**
- "Within X weeks/months" format

**Examples:**
- "Winners announced November 27, 2025"
- "Finalists announced by the 15th of the following month"
- "Final pitch competition September 10, 2026"

**AI Rule:** Never explain, just state timing. For competitions, turnaround time is usually an announcement timeline, not an approval timeline.

## Section 4: Eligibility Criteria

Only two sub-sections for this grant type: Eligible Applicants and Ineligible Applicants. No Projects, Activities, or Expenses sections.

Include each sub-section only if clearly cited in program guidelines or webpage. Never infer ineligibility.

### 4.1 Eligible Applicants

**Aim:** Define who can enter the competition.

**Length & depth:**
- 4–8 bullet points
- Include:
  - business type
  - location
  - business registration
  - business stage
  - sector
  - revenue threshold
  - operational history
  - incorporation status
  - employee size restrictions
  - industry or strategic focus

**Language style:**
- Clear, eligibility-driven
- No explanation or examples unless necessary
- Include additional clarifications as a "Note" bullet in *italics*

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points ending with semicolons
- Group logically

**Examples:**
- women-owned businesses
- early-stage technology startups
- pre-Series A AgTech startups
- businesses with strong recent growth
- businesses in creative, service-based, or social impact sectors

**AI Rule:** Focus strictly on employer eligibility.

### 4.2 Ineligible Applicants

Same aim, structure, and formatting as Eligible Applicants. Include only if clearly cited.

## Section 5: Program Details

**Aim:** Explain how the competition works. Includes:
- application deadline
- entry fee (if applicable)
- number of winners
- finalist stages
- round details / timeline
- pitch video requirements
- public voting
- live pitch competition
- evaluation criteria (if applicable)
- mentorship / workshops
- sponsor or administrator
- reporting or promotional obligations

**Length & depth:**
- 5–15 concise bullet points

**Language style:**
- Operational, structured

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points ending with semicolons
- Group by:
  - structure (phases, streams)
  - timelines
  - limits (applications, duration)

**AI Rule:** This section explains the competition mechanics.

## Section 7: Application Process

### 7.1 Condensed Grant Steps

1–5 steps in plain business language. Typical flow:

1. Confirm eligibility and apply;
2. If shortlisted, submit pitch video or additional materials;
3. Complete written questions, pitch deck, or business summary;
4. Participate in judging, public voting, or live pitch event;
5. Winners are announced and complete reporting/promotional obligations.

**AI Rule:** For competitions, include shortlisting, judging, and finalist requirements — not claims or reimbursement.

### 7.2 Extended Grant Steps

Same 1–5 steps with detail. Keep concise; use bullet points.

For each step, cover relevant items from:
- Pre-application prep work (budget, assessment, pre-eligibility survey)
- Timing context (Step 1 approval within X weeks, then Step 2)
- Documentation and information required
- Obtaining documents like quotes or letters of support
- Post-approval claims and reporting process and frequency

## Type-Specific Reminders

- **Prize Value, not Grant Value.** Use *Prize Value* or *Award Value* in Section 3.2.
- **No Projects/Activities/Expenses sections.** Competitions recognize achievement; they don't fund specific work scopes. Stop the eligibility section after Eligible/Ineligible Applicants.
- **Hard deadlines are the norm.** Competitions usually have a single application deadline, not rolling intake. Make the deadline prominent in Program Details.
- **Multi-stage structure.** Application → shortlist → pitch → winners is the typical flow. Reflect this in Application Process.
- **Reporting and promotion.** Winners often have to participate in marketing or reporting activities. Surface this in Program Details when cited.
- **Entry fees.** Some competitions charge to enter. If cited, surface in Section 1 (Applicant Contribution %) and Program Details.
