# Grant Card Writing — Market Expansion

Format spec for **Market Expansion** grants. Load this sub-skill after detecting that the program funds domestic or international market entry, trade shows, marketing, or sales growth.

This sub-skill assumes the agent has already loaded the parent `OVERVIEW` skill and applied its general rules. Everything here is type-specific.

---

## What Market Expansion Grants Consistently Emphasize

- **Market growth activity** — expanding into new markets, growing sales, diversifying geography
- **Domestic vs. international scope** — most programs target one explicitly
- **Reimbursement-based funding** — applicants spend first, claim later
- **Project streams** — programs often have multiple streams (planning, implementation, trade shows, export development)
- **Minimum spend thresholds** — small projects often don't qualify

These themes should appear naturally across the Preview, Grant Overview, Eligible Projects, and Eligible Activities sections.

## Section 2: Preview Description

**Aim:** Explain what kind of expansion the program supports (domestic vs. international), including:
- What market growth activity is being supported
- Where growth is happening (domestic / international / export / new markets)
- Why the program exists

**Length & depth:**
- 2–3 lines maximum (1–2 sentences)
- Paragraph format, not bullets
- No dollar amounts, no percentages, no eligibility details, no deadlines, no process, no project examples, no program history, no government commitments

**Language style:**
- Plain business language ("Layman's language")
- Vocabulary: expand, diversify, export, enter new markets, increase sales, trade resilience
- No jargon, no program mechanics

**Formatting:**
- Single short paragraph
- No bullets

**AI Rule:** Describe where the business is expanding + how the program helps them get there. The Preview answers "what is this program?" — nothing else. If a sentence contains a number, a percentage, a date, or the word "eligible," remove it.

## Section 3: General Requirements

### 3.1 Grant Overview

**Aim:** Expand the Preview by adding the key details an applicant needs to decide if the program is a fit for them. Answers: "What is this program, and is it a fit for me?" For Market Expansion programs, explain the funding model, project streams, and broad target audience.

**Length & depth:**
- 3–4 lines maximum (2–4 sentences)
- Must include:
  - funding % (per-applicant coverage)
  - maximum contribution (per-applicant value, not total program budget)
  - major project streams/categories
  - broad applicant type
- Add additional context only when relevant: expected outcomes

**Language style:**
- Plain language. Market expansion programs frequently have multiple project streams:
  - planning
  - implementation
  - trade shows
  - export development
  - equipment tied to expansion
- Informational, not instructional
- Not narrative history — do not describe when the program launched or government priorities

**Formatting:**
- Paragraph format
- Followed by "Grant Value" as a separate line or block

**Critical distinction — total program budget vs. grant value:**
- The program's total budget (e.g., "$291 million initiative") is rarely useful to an individual applicant.
- The grant value is what an individual applicant can receive.
- Always report grant value to the applicant. Never use total program budget as the headline figure.

**AI Rule:** Explain what kind of growth activities the funding supports. If a sentence describes program mechanics, history, or government goals rather than what's offered to the applicant, remove it.

### 3.2 Grant Value

**Aim:** Clearly quantify the financial or in-kind support. Distinguish averages vs. caps vs. cost-share.

**Length & depth:**
- As short as possible
- Bullets in this order:
  - % contribution (coverage percentage of eligible costs)
  - maximum funding amount per applicant
  - average funding (if relevant — add NOTE if averages differ from caps)
  - minimum spend thresholds (if applicable)
  - multiple caps by activity type (mention in Expenses section if needed)
  - additional funding for specific project types or applicant groups (if applicable)
  - applicant contribution requirement (if applicable)

**Language style:**
- Numeric and precise
- No explanation unless necessary

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points or short lines
- Use consistent wording across cards: "Up to X% of eligible costs," "Up to $X per [project/applicant/year]"

**Critical distinction — program budget vs. applicant value:**
- Report grant value to the applicant, not the program's total budget.
- If the source material only gives a total program budget without per-applicant figures, state that the per-applicant maximum is not specified in source material. Do not divide, estimate, or infer.
- If a value isn't in the source material, omit the bullet. Do not fabricate.

**AI Rule:** Market Expansion programs often have:
- minimum spend thresholds
- multiple caps by activity type (mention in Expenses section if needed)
- reimbursement models

### 3.3 Turnaround Time

**Aim:** Set expectations on how long approval takes.

**Length & depth:**
- One short line

**Language style:**
- Direct and factual
- Can include milestone dates (EOI, proposal, decision)

**Formatting:**
- "Within X weeks/months" format

**AI Rule:** Include timeline context if applicable.

## Section 4: Eligibility Criteria

Include each sub-section only if clearly cited in program guidelines or webpage. Never infer ineligibility.

### 4.1 Eligible Applicants

**Aim:** Define who can receive funding for expansion activities.

**Length & depth:**
- 4–8 bullet points
- Include:
  - business type
  - location
  - size/revenue
  - sector or strategic focus

**Language style:**
- Clear, eligibility-driven
- No explanation or examples unless necessary
- Include additional clarifications as a "Note" bullet

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points ending with semicolons
- Group logically (core eligibility → additional criteria → priorities)

**AI Rule:** Only include decision-making criteria. Go beyond "who they are" — include their ability to deliver the project and fit for the program mandate.

### 4.2 Ineligible Applicants

Same aim, structure, and formatting as Eligible Applicants. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.3 Eligible Projects

**Aim:** Define what a fundable project looks like.

**Length & depth:**
- 5–10 bullets

**Language style:**
- Plain business language

**Formatting:**
- Start with high-level criteria, then examples
- Must show:
  - market growth intent
  - clear target market
  - measurable expansion outcomes

**AI Rule:** This section defines what gets funded, not just activities.

### 4.4 Ineligible Projects

Same structure, language, and formatting as Eligible Projects. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.5 Eligible Activities

**Aim:** Describe the work involved in expanding into new markets.

**Length & depth:**
- 5–8 bullets
- Moderate length (not exhaustive unless necessary)
- Include additional clarifications as a "Note" bullet

**Language style:**
- Functional verbs: attending, exhibiting, marketing, exporting
- Activities consistently include:
  - market research
  - trade show attendance
  - marketing campaigns
  - translation and certification
  - export readiness work

**Formatting:**
- Group by theme if complex

**AI Rule:** Focus on market-entry and growth activities, not operational or production work.

### 4.6 Ineligible Activities

Same aim, structure, and formatting as Eligible Activities. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.7 Eligible Expenses

**Aim:** Define what costs are covered.

**Length & depth:**
- Bullet points

**Language style:**
- Specific and practical

**Usually includes:**
- travel
- trade show costs
- consultants
- translation
- legal support
- certification
- marketing activities

**Common exclusions:**
- salaries
- overhead
- capital costs
- inventory
- product development

**AI Rule:** Translate policy/program language into clear business terms. Separate market-entry costs from operational costs.

### 4.8 Ineligible Expenses

Same structure and formatting as Eligible Expenses. Include only if clearly cited.

## Section 6: Best Practices and Forms

### 6.1 Eligible Best Practices

**Aim:** Highlight rules that impact funding success, compliance, and competitiveness. Helps determine a suitable project for this program.

**Length & depth:**
- 2–6 bullets

**Language style:**
- Advisory tone

**Typical content:**
- stacking rules
- cost-share expectations
- eligibility nuances
- program retroactivity rules
- private funding leverage
- partnerships
- geographic requirements
- online portal logins required
- application strategy
- supplementary documentation
- claims terms and financial commitment requirements
- documentation and reporting post-approval

**AI Rule:** Focus on funding logic, not application tactics.

### 6.2 Process Best Practices

**Aim:** Help applicants navigate the process more effectively.

**Length & depth:**
- 2–5 bullets

**Language style:**
- Practical and strategic

**Typical content:**
- focus on execution
- prepare budgets early
- gather financials
- build realistic timelines
- maintain claim documentation

**AI Rule:** Focus on how to apply better, not eligibility.

### 6.3 Forms

Direct access to application materials. No descriptions, just links.

- Program Website: [URL from source material]
- Program Guidelines: [URL from source material]
- Application Portal: [URL from source material]

**Rules:**
- Use these three labels exactly (Title Case). Do not invent alternative labels (e.g., "FCM Portal," "Eligible Costs Document," "FAQ").
- If a URL is in the source material, include it as a clickable link.
- If a URL is not in the source material, omit that bullet entirely. Do not write "[click here]," "TBD," or any placeholder.
- Do not add new rows beyond these three labels, even if the source material contains additional resources.

**AI Rule:** Exactly three possible rows. Include each only if you have a real URL from the source. No descriptions, no placeholders, no extras.

## Section 7: Application Process

### 7.1 Condensed Grant Steps

1–5 steps in plain business language without much detail.

**AI Rule:** Keep generic unless program-specific steps are clearly defined.

### 7.2 Extended Grant Steps

Same 1–5 steps with detail. Keep concise; use bullet points.

For each step, cover relevant items from:
- Pre-application prep work (budget, assessment, pre-eligibility survey)
- Timing context (Step 1 approval within X weeks, then Step 2)
- Documentation and information required
- Obtaining documents like quotes or letters of support
- Post-approval claims and reporting process and frequency

## Type-Specific Reminders

- **Domestic vs. international.** Most programs target one. Make the scope unmistakable in the Preview and Grant Overview.
- **Reimbursement is the norm.** Applicants spend first, claim later. Surface this in Program Details and Best Practices.
- **Project streams matter.** Many programs have planning vs. implementation vs. trade show streams with different funding caps. Reflect the stream structure in Grant Overview and Program Details.
- **Common exclusions are operational costs.** Salaries, overhead, inventory, and product development are usually excluded. Make this explicit in Ineligible Expenses if cited.
