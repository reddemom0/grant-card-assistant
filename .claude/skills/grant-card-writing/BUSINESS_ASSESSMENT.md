# Grant Card Writing — Business Assessment / Planning & Coaching

Format spec for **Business Assessment, Planning & Coaching** grants. Load this sub-skill after detecting that the program funds advisory services, planning, coaching, assessments, or audits.

This sub-skill assumes the agent has already loaded the parent `OVERVIEW` skill and applied its general rules. Everything here is type-specific.

---

## What Business Assessment Grants Consistently Emphasize

- **Advisory and consulting support** — third-party experts, specialized advice
- **Planning and strategy** — not execution-heavy funding
- **Decision-making tools** — risk, growth, IP, scaling
- **In-kind vs. cash support** — mentorship programs often provide hours rather than dollars
- **Output is a plan or report**, not capital or hiring

These themes should appear naturally across the Preview, Grant Overview, and Eligible Activities sections.

## Section 2: Preview Description

**Aim:** Explain what kind of business support or planning the program funds.

**Length & depth:**
- 2–3 lines maximum (1–2 sentences)
- Paragraph format, not bullets
- No dollar amounts, no percentages, no eligibility details, no deadlines, no process, no project examples, no program history, no government commitments

**Language style:**
- Plain business language ("Layman's language")
- Focus on the type of assessment or planning support
- Often includes the area of focus (e.g., digital readiness, market research, strategic planning)
- No jargon, no program mechanics

**Formatting:**
- Single short paragraph
- No bullets

**AI Rule:** For this grant type, the preview usually highlights:
- type of support (advisory, mentorship, planning, IP, etc.)
- core objective (decision-making, growth, IP protection, scaling)

The Preview answers "what is this program?" — nothing else. If a sentence contains a number, a percentage, a date, or the word "eligible," remove it.

## Section 3: General Requirements

### 3.1 Grant Overview

**Aim:** Expand the Preview by adding the key details an applicant needs to decide if the program is a fit for them. Answers: "What is this program, and is it a fit for me?" For Business Assessment programs, anchor the overview in advisory scope + funding scale + intended deliverable.

**Length & depth:**
- 3–4 lines maximum (2–4 sentences)
- Must include:
  - funding range or typical size (per-applicant value, not total program budget)
  - type of advisory work covered
  - intended deliverable (plan, assessment, report)
- Add additional context only when relevant: contribution percentage, expected outcomes, who it's for

**Language style:**
- Plain business language ("Layman's language")
- Informational, not instructional
- Not narrative history — do not describe when the program launched or government priorities

**Formatting:**
- Paragraph format
- Followed by "Grant Value" as a separate line or block

**Critical distinction — total program budget vs. grant value:**
- The program's total budget (e.g., "$291 million initiative") is rarely useful to an individual applicant.
- The grant value is what an individual applicant can receive.
- Always report grant value to the applicant. Never use total program budget as the headline figure.

**AI Rule:** Anchor the program in advisory scope + funding scale + deliverable. If a sentence describes program mechanics, history, or government goals rather than what's offered to the applicant, remove it.

### 3.2 Grant Value

**Aim:** Clearly quantify the financial support and any caps.

**Length & depth:**
- As short as possible
- Bullets in this order:
  - % contribution (coverage percentage of eligible costs)
  - maximum funding amount per applicant
  - per-hour or per-project caps (common in this grant type)
  - additional funding for specific project types or applicant groups (if applicable)
  - applicant contribution requirement (if applicable)
  - NOTE if caps differ from typical use

**Language style:**
- Numeric and precise
- No explanation unless necessary

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points or short lines
- Use consistent wording across cards: "Up to X% of eligible costs," "Up to $X per [project/applicant/hour]"

**Critical distinction — program budget vs. applicant value:**
- Report grant value to the applicant, not the program's total budget.
- If the source material only gives a total program budget without per-applicant figures, state that the per-applicant maximum is not specified in source material. Do not divide, estimate, or infer.
- If a value isn't in the source material, omit the bullet. Do not fabricate.

**AI Rule:** The value of mentorship programs is estimated based on the number of hours of professional guidance provided. Some programs estimate this value, others don't. If the value is known and listed on the program's website, add it to the grant card. If the value is unknown, use: *"This program provides in-kind support only (no direct funding)."*

### 3.3 Turnaround Time

**Aim:** Set expectations on how long approval takes.

**Length & depth:**
- One short line

**Language style:**
- Direct and factual

**Formatting:**
- "Within X weeks/months" format

**AI Rule:** Include timeline context if multi-stage.

## Section 4: Eligibility Criteria

Include each sub-section only if clearly cited in program guidelines or webpage. Never infer ineligibility.

### 4.1 Eligible Applicants

**Aim:** Define who can engage advisory services through this program.

**Length & depth:**
- 4–8 bullet points
- Include:
  - business type
  - location
  - size/revenue
  - sector or strategic focus
  - business stage (startup, growth, established)

**Language style:**
- Clear, eligibility-driven
- No explanation or examples unless necessary
- Include additional clarifications as a "Note" bullet

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points ending with semicolons
- Group logically (core eligibility → additional criteria → priorities)

**AI Rule:** Focus on business readiness criteria — programs in this category often target businesses at a specific maturity stage.

### 4.2 Ineligible Applicants

Same aim, structure, and formatting as Eligible Applicants. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.3 Eligible Activities

**Aim:** Clarify what the program will actually support at the activity level.

**Length & depth:**
- Bullet list
- Moderate length (not exhaustive unless necessary)
- Include additional clarifications as a "Note" bullet

**Language style:**
- Functional verbs: developing, assessing, implementing
- Activities consistently include:
  - market research
  - strategic planning
  - feasibility analysis
  - readiness assessments
  - business plan development

**Formatting:**
- Group by theme if complex (e.g., streams, tiers)

**AI Rule:** Focus on types of work funded, not outcomes.

### 4.4 Ineligible Activities

Same aim, structure, and formatting as Eligible Activities. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.5 Eligible Expenses

**Aim:** Define what costs are covered.

**Length & depth:**
- Bullet points
- More detailed than activities

**Language style:**
- Specific and practical

**Formatting:**
- Include examples where helpful
- Keep long exclusions grouped logically

**AI Rule:** Translate policy/program language into clear business terms.

### 4.6 Ineligible Expenses

Same structure and formatting as Eligible Expenses. Include only if clearly cited.

## Section 5: Program Details

**Aim:** Explain how the program actually runs. Includes:
- phases (Discovery, Traction, Streams, etc.)
- timelines
- constraints (1 application, deadlines)
- evaluation criteria

**Length & depth:**
- 5–15 concise bullet points

**Language style:**
- Operational, structured
- Vary sentence structure across bullets. Avoid stacking many short, formulaic openings ("Projects must...", "Funding is...", "Applicants must..."). Combine related rules into flowing phrasing where natural. Write like a knowledgeable consultant briefing a client, not a policy document.

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points ending with semicolons
- Group by:
  - structure (phases, streams)
  - timelines
  - limits (applications, duration)

**AI Rule:** This is the "how it works" section, not eligibility or funding.

## Section 6: Best Practices and Forms

### 6.1 Eligible Best Practices

**Aim:** Highlight rules that impact funding success or compliance.

**Length & depth:**
- 2–5 bullets

**Language style:**
- Advisory tone

**Typical content:**
- stacking rules
- cost-share expectations
- eligibility nuances
- program retroactivity rules
- what helps determine a suitable project for this program

**AI Rule:** Focus on funding logic, not application tactics.

### 6.2 Process Best Practices

**Aim:** Help applicants navigate the process more effectively.

**Length & depth:**
- 2–5 bullets

**Language style:**
- Practical and strategic

**Typical content:**
- sequencing advice (e.g., apply to Stream 2 first)
- preparation tips
- online portal logins required
- application strategy
- supplementary documentation
- claims terms and financial commitment requirements
- documentation and reporting post-approval

**AI Rule:** Focus on how to apply better, not eligibility.

### 6.3 Forms

Direct access to application materials. No descriptions, just links.

- Program Website: [URL from source material]
- Program Guidelines: [URL from source material]
- Application Portal: [URL from source material]

**Rules:**
- Use these three labels exactly (Title Case). Do not invent alternative labels (e.g., "FCM Portal," "Eligible Costs Document," "FAQ").
- If a URL is in the source material, include it as a clickable link.
- If a URL is not in the source material, omit that bullet entirely. Do not write "[click here]," "TBD," or any placeholder.
- Do not add new rows beyond these three labels, even if the source material contains additional resources.

**AI Rule:** Exactly three possible rows. Include each only if you have a real URL from the source. No descriptions, no placeholders, no extras.

## Section 7: Application Process

### 7.1 Condensed Grant Steps

1–5 steps in plain business language. Generic flow:

1. Apply;
2. (Optional) Additional steps;
3. Approval;
4. Completion;
5. Reimbursement.

**AI Rule:** Keep generic unless program-specific steps are clearly defined.

### 7.2 Extended Grant Steps

Same 1–5 steps with detail. Keep concise; use bullet points.

For each step, cover relevant items from:
- Pre-application prep work (budget, assessment, pre-eligibility survey)
- Timing context (Step 1 approval within X weeks, then Step 2)
- Documentation and information required
- Obtaining documents like quotes or letters of support
- Post-approval claims and reporting process and frequency

## Type-Specific Reminders

- **In-kind support is common.** Many mentorship and coaching programs provide hours rather than cash. Reflect this clearly in Grant Value — use the "in-kind support only" wording when no dollar value is published.
- **The deliverable matters.** Programs fund specific outputs (plans, assessments, reports). Make the deliverable clear in Grant Overview.
- **No implementation funding.** This grant type funds *planning* the work, not *doing* the work. If the source material conflates the two, separate them clearly.
- **Stream structure.** Programs in this category sometimes have phases or streams (Discovery, Traction, etc.). Reflect this in Grant Value and Program Details.
