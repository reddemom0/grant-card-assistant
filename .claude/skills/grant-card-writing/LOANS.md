# Grant Card Writing — Loans

Format spec for **Loan (Repayable Contribution)** programs. Load this sub-skill after detecting that the program offers repayable financing rather than non-repayable grants.

This sub-skill assumes the agent has already loaded the parent `OVERVIEW` skill and applied its general rules. Everything here is type-specific.

---

## What Loan Programs Consistently Emphasize

- **Financing, not reimbursement.** Funding must be repaid, fully or partially.
- **Repayment structure** — interest rates, deferral periods, amortization
- **Forgivable portions** — many loan programs include a forgivable component if certain conditions are met
- **Underwriting and due diligence** — financial review is typically required
- **Mixed support** — some programs combine repayable financing with non-repayable grants

The defining question: *Is the applicant receiving repayable financing, forgivable financing, or a mix of repayable and non-repayable support?*

These themes should appear naturally across the Preview, Program Overview, and Loan Value sections.

## Section 2: Preview Description

**Aim:** Explain what the loan supports, who it is for, and the business or project outcome.

**Length & depth:**
- 2–3 lines maximum (1–2 sentences)
- Paragraph format, not bullets
- No dollar amounts, no percentages, no eligibility details, no deadlines, no process, no project examples, no program history, no government commitments

**Language style:**
- Plain business language ("Layman's language")
- Outcome-focused (what the business gets)
- No jargon, no program mechanics

**Typical wording:**
- "provides loans to support..."
- "offers low-cost financing for..."
- "provides repayable contributions for..."

**Formatting:**
- Single paragraph
- No bullets

**AI Rule:** Describe the financing purpose + target applicant + intended project or business outcome. The Preview answers "what is this program?" — nothing else. If a sentence contains a number, a percentage, a date, or the word "eligible," remove it.

## Section 3: General Requirements

### 3.1 Program Overview

**Aim:** Expand the Preview by adding the key details an applicant needs to decide if the program is a fit for them. Answers: "What is this program, and is it a fit for me?" For Loan programs, anchor the overview in funding structure + applicant scope + program intent.

**Length & depth:**
- 3–4 lines maximum (2–4 sentences)
- Includes:
  - funding model (%, max $, interest rates)
  - key eligibility signal
  - program intent
- Add additional context only when relevant: contribution percentage, expected outcomes

**Language style:**
- Still plain, but slightly more detailed
- Informational, not instructional
- Not narrative history — do not describe when the program launched or government priorities

**Formatting:**
- Paragraph format
- Followed by "Loan Value" as a separate line or block

**Critical distinction — total loan envelope vs. loan value:**
- The program's total loan envelope (e.g., "$500 million loan fund") is rarely useful to an individual applicant.
- The loan value is what an individual applicant can receive (e.g., "loans up to $5M at 0% interest").
- Always report loan value to the applicant. Never use total fund size as the headline figure.

**AI Rule:** Expand the preview by adding who it's for, how much funding is provided, and how funding works — without going into lists. If a sentence describes program mechanics, history, or government goals rather than what's offered to the applicant, remove it.

### 3.2 Loan Value

**Aim:** Clearly explain the financing structure.

**Length & depth:**
- As short as possible
- Bullets in this order:
  - minimum and maximum loan amount (per applicant)
  - repayable vs. non-repayable portion
  - forgivable portion (if applicable)
  - loan-to-cost percentage
  - cost-share percentage
  - repayment deferral
  - interest-free or low-interest terms
  - collateral / personal guarantee requirements (if stated)
  - additional terms for specific project types or applicant groups (if applicable)

**Language style:**
- Numeric and precise
- No explanation unless necessary

**Formatting:**
- Use bullets, especially when different applicant types receive different support
- Separate streams/tiers clearly
- Use consistent wording across cards: "Loans up to $X," "Up to X% loan-to-cost," "0% interest for first X months"

**Critical distinction — total loan fund vs. applicant loan value:**
- Report loan value to the applicant, not the total fund size.
- If the source material only gives a total fund size without per-applicant figures, state that the per-applicant maximum is not specified in source material. Do not divide, estimate, or infer.
- If a value isn't in the source material, omit the bullet. Do not fabricate.

**AI Rule:** Do not label this "Grant Value" unless the program includes a non-repayable grant. Use **Loan Value**, **Funding Value**, or **Repayable Contribution Value**.

### 3.3 Turnaround Time

**Aim:** Set expectations on how long approval takes.

**Length & depth:**
- One short line

**Language style:**
- Direct and factual

**Formatting:**
- "Within X weeks/months" format
- "Approximately X months"
- Use "To be confirmed" if date is not known

**AI Rule:** Loan programs often have underwriting or due diligence. If review time includes this, mention it briefly in Program Details, not here.

## Section 4: Eligibility Criteria

Include each sub-section only if clearly cited in program guidelines or webpage. Never infer ineligibility.

### 4.1 Eligible Applicants

**Aim:** Define who can access the financing.

**Length & depth:**
- 4–8 bullet points
- Include:
  - business type
  - location
  - business registration
  - operational history
  - sector restrictions
  - employee size restrictions
  - industry or strategic focus
  - repayment capacity

**Language style:**
- Clear, eligibility-driven
- No explanation or examples unless necessary
- Include additional clarifications as a "Note" bullet in *italics*

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points ending with semicolons
- Group logically (core eligibility → additional criteria → priorities)

**AI Rule:** For loans, include financial capacity and repayment readiness when available.

### 4.2 Ineligible Applicants

Same aim, structure, and formatting as Eligible Applicants. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.3 Eligible Projects

**Aim:** Explain what the loan can finance.

**Length & depth:**
- 5–10 bullets
- Often grouped by stream

**Language style:**
- Technical but clear

**Formatting:**
- Start with high-level criteria, then examples

**Common project types:**
- new construction
- facility expansion
- building retrofits
- equipment purchases
- accessibility upgrades
- energy efficiency retrofits
- vehicle or fleet equipment
- automation and robotics
- renewable energy systems

**AI Rule:** Because loans can overlap with other grant types, define the project type first, then explain the financing.

### 4.4 Ineligible Projects

Same structure, language, and formatting as Eligible Projects. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.5 Eligible Activities

**Aim:** Describe the actions the applicant can carry out with the financing.

**Length & depth:**
- 5–8 bullets
- Moderate length (not exhaustive unless necessary)
- Include additional clarifications as a "Note" bullet in *italics*

**Language style:**
- Functional verbs: developing, assessing, implementing

**Formatting:**
- Group by theme if complex

**AI Rule:** Include this section when the program clearly funds specific activities. If the program is structured around eligible projects/costs only, this section can be omitted.

### 4.6 Ineligible Activities

Same aim, structure, and formatting as Eligible Activities. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.7 Eligible Expenses

**Aim:** List costs the loan or repayable contribution may cover.

**Length & depth:**
- Bullet points
- More detailed than activities

**Language style:**
- Specific and practical

**Formatting:**
- Include examples where helpful

**AI Rule:** Eligible expenses must be directly tied to the capital asset, project, or installation.

### 4.8 Ineligible Expenses

Same structure and formatting as Eligible Expenses. Include only if clearly cited.

## Section 6: Best Practices and Forms

### 6.1 Eligible Best Practices

**Aim:** Highlight rules or strategy points that improve eligibility, funding success, or compliance.

**Length & depth:**
- 2–5 bullets

**Language style:**
- Advisory tone

**Typical content:**
- stacking rules
- eligibility nuances
- what helps determine a suitable applicant

**AI Rule:** Focus on eligibility and financing readiness, not just application tips.

### 6.2 Process Best Practices

**Aim:** Help applicants navigate the process more effectively.

**Length & depth:**
- 2–5 bullets

**Language style:**
- Practical and strategic

**AI Rule:** Loan applications require more proof than grant applications. Emphasize documentation quality.

### 6.3 Forms

Direct access to application materials. No descriptions, just links.

- Program Website: [URL from source material]
- Program Guidelines: [URL from source material]
- Application Portal: [URL from source material]

**Rules:**
- Use these three labels exactly (Title Case). Do not invent alternative labels (e.g., "FCM Portal," "Eligible Costs Document," "FAQ").
- If a URL is in the source material, include it as a clickable link.
- If a URL is not in the source material, omit that bullet entirely. Do not write "[click here]," "TBD," or any placeholder.
- Do not add new rows beyond these three labels, even if the source material contains additional resources.

**AI Rule:** Exactly three possible rows. Include each only if you have a real URL from the source. No descriptions, no placeholders, no extras.

## Section 7: Application Process

### 7.1 Condensed Grant Steps

1–5 steps in plain business language. Typical loan flow:

1. Confirm eligibility and project fit;
2. Prepare business plan, financial statements, budget, and supporting documents;
3. Submit eligibility self-assessment or initial application, if required;
4. Complete detailed application and due diligence/underwriting;
5. Sign loan, contribution, or funding agreement; receive disbursement; repay according to terms.

**AI Rule:** For loan programs, always include due diligence/underwriting/agreement signing when applicable.

### 7.2 Extended Grant Steps

Same 1–5 steps with detail. Keep concise; use bullet points.

For each step, cover relevant items from:
- Pre-application prep work (budget, assessment, pre-eligibility survey)
- Timing context (Step 1 approval within X weeks, then Step 2)
- Documentation and information required
- Obtaining documents like quotes or letters of support
- Post-approval claims and reporting process and frequency

## Type-Specific Reminders

- **Loan Value, not Grant Value.** Unless the program has a non-repayable component, use *Loan Value*, *Funding Value*, or *Repayable Contribution Value* in Section 3.2.
- **Forgivable portions.** Many programs include a forgivable component if conditions are met (job creation, milestone hit, retention period). Make this prominent in Loan Value when cited.
- **Underwriting and due diligence.** Loan programs typically require financial review the applicant must prepare for. Surface in Program Details and Process Best Practices.
- **Collateral and personal guarantees.** Common in commercial loan programs but not universal. Include only if cited.
- **Loans can overlap with other types.** A loan can finance Capital Cost projects, Market Expansion activities, etc. Define the project type first, then explain the financing structure.
