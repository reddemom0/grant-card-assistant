# Grant Card Writing — Investment

Format spec for **Investment** programs. Load this sub-skill after detecting that the program offers equity investment in exchange for company shares.

This sub-skill assumes the agent has already loaded the parent `OVERVIEW` skill and applied its general rules. Everything here is type-specific.

---

## What Investment Programs Consistently Emphasize

- **Equity in exchange for funding** — applicants give up shares, not just repay
- **Growth readiness and investability** — programs target companies with traction
- **Investment committee review** — applications go through formal investment review
- **Milestone-based reporting** — investors track growth metrics over time
- **Matching private capital** — many programs require co-investment from private investors

These themes should appear naturally across the Preview, Program Overview, and Eligibility sections.

## Section 2: Preview Description

**Aim:** Explain what kind of company the program invests in and what stage of growth it supports.

**Length & depth:**
- 2–3 lines maximum (1–2 sentences)
- Paragraph format, not bullets
- No dollar amounts, no percentages, no eligibility details, no deadlines, no process, no project examples, no program history, no government commitments

**Language style:**
- Plain business language ("Layman's language")
- Outcome-focused (what the company gets — growth capital, validation, partnership)
- No jargon, no program mechanics

**Formatting:**
- Single paragraph
- No bullets

**AI Rule:** Describe the company stage + investment focus + growth purpose. The Preview answers "what is this program?" — nothing else. If a sentence contains a number, a percentage, a date, or the word "eligible," remove it.

## Section 3: General Requirements

### 3.1 Program Overview

**Aim:** Expand the Preview by adding the key details an applicant needs to decide if the program is a fit for them. Answers: "What is this program, and is it a fit for me?" For Investment programs, anchor the overview in investment structure + program scope + applicant fit.

**Length & depth:**
- 3–4 lines maximum (2–4 sentences)
- Includes:
  - investment model (direct equity, convertible debenture, SAFE, etc.)
  - investment range (per-applicant value, not total fund size)
  - key eligibility signal
  - program intent
- Add additional context only when relevant: matching requirements, expected outcomes

**Language style:**
- Still plain, but slightly more detailed
- Informational, not instructional
- Not narrative history — do not describe when the program launched or government priorities

**Formatting:**
- Paragraph format
- Followed by "Investment Value" as a separate line or block

**Critical distinction — total fund size vs. investment value:**
- The program's total fund size (e.g., "$200 million fund") is rarely useful to an individual applicant.
- The investment value is what an individual company can receive (e.g., "$500K–$5M direct equity per company").
- Always report investment value to the applicant. Never use total fund size as the headline figure.

**AI Rule:** Expand the preview by adding who it's for, how much investment is provided, and the investment structure — without going into lists. If a sentence describes program mechanics, history, or government goals rather than what's offered to the applicant, remove it.

### 3.2 Investment Value

**Aim:** Clearly quantify the financial support and investment structure.

**Length & depth:**
- As short as possible
- Bullets in this order:
  - investment range — minimum and maximum (per applicant)
  - equity percentage taken
  - matching private capital requirements
  - convertible debenture or SAFE terms (if applicable)
  - follow-on investment terms (if applicable)
  - additional terms for specific project types or applicant groups (if applicable)

**Language style:**
- Numeric and precise
- No explanation unless necessary

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points or short lines
- Separate streams/tiers clearly
- Use consistent wording across cards: "Investments from $X to $Y," "Up to X% equity," "X:1 matching private capital required"

**Critical distinction — total fund size vs. applicant investment value:**
- Report investment value to the applicant, not the total fund size.
- If the source material only gives a total fund size without per-applicant figures, state that the per-applicant maximum is not specified in source material. Do not divide, estimate, or infer.
- If a value isn't in the source material, omit the bullet. Do not fabricate.

**AI Rule:** Use **Investment Value**, not Grant Value, unless the program is truly non-repayable.

### 3.3 Turnaround Time

**Aim:** Set expectations on how long approval takes.

**Length & depth:**
- One short line

**Language style:**
- Direct and factual

**Formatting:**
- Common examples: "Initial response within 2 weeks", "3 months"
- Use "To be confirmed" if date is not known

**AI Rule:** Never explain, just state timing.

## Section 4: Eligibility Criteria

Include each sub-section only if clearly cited in program guidelines or webpage. Never infer ineligibility.

### 4.1 Eligible Applicants

**Aim:** Define which businesses or organizations can receive investment.

**Length & depth:**
- 4–8 bullet points
- Include:
  - business type
  - location
  - company stage
  - traction
  - revenue or market validation
  - investor readiness
  - private matching capital
  - incubator/accelerator affiliation
  - industry or strategic focus

**Language style:**
- Clear, eligibility-driven
- No explanation or examples unless necessary
- Include additional clarifications as a "Note" bullet in *italics*

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points ending with semicolons
- Group logically (core eligibility → additional criteria → priorities)

**AI Rule:** For investment cards, focus on growth readiness and investability, not only basic eligibility.

### 4.2 Ineligible Applicants

Same aim, structure, and formatting as Eligible Applicants. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.3 Eligible Projects

**Aim:** Explain what the investment can support.

**Length & depth:**
- 4–8 bullets
- Often grouped by stream

**Language style:**
- Technical but clear

**Formatting:**
- Start with high-level criteria, then examples

**AI Rule:** Investment use should connect to growth milestones, not ordinary expenses.

### 4.4 Ineligible Projects

Same structure, language, and formatting as Eligible Projects. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.5 Eligible Activities

**Aim:** Describe the actions funded through the investment.

**Length & depth:**
- 3–7 bullets
- Moderate length (not exhaustive unless necessary)
- Include additional clarifications as a "Note" bullet in *italics*

**Language style:**
- Functional verbs: developing, scaling, hiring, expanding

**Formatting:**
- Group by theme if complex

**AI Rule:** Use this section when the program describes specific business-building activities.

### 4.6 Ineligible Activities

Same aim, structure, and formatting as Eligible Activities. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.7 Eligible Expenses

**Aim:** Clarify how investment proceeds may be spent.

**Length & depth:**
- Bullet points
- More detailed than activities

**Language style:**
- Specific and practical

**Formatting:**
- Include examples where helpful

**AI Rule:** Expenses should support the company's next growth or investment milestone.

### 4.8 Ineligible Expenses

Same structure and formatting as Eligible Expenses. Include only if clearly cited.

## Section 5: Program Details

**Aim:** Explain how the investment process works. Includes:
- direct investment vs. fund investment
- investment committee review
- matching private capital
- due diligence
- investment agreement
- convertible debenture / SAFE terms
- activation requirements
- reporting period
- milestone requirements
- impact metrics
- evaluation criteria

**Length & depth:**
- 5–15 concise bullet points

**Language style:**
- Operational, structured
- Vary sentence structure across bullets. Avoid stacking many short, formulaic openings ("Companies must...", "Investments are...", "Founders must..."). Combine related rules into flowing phrasing where natural. Write like a knowledgeable consultant briefing a client, not a policy document.

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points ending with semicolons
- Group by:
  - structure (phases, streams)
  - timelines
  - limits (applications, duration)

**AI Rule:** This section should explain investment mechanics, not just eligibility.

## Section 6: Best Practices and Forms

### 6.1 Eligible Best Practices

**Aim:** Highlight rules or strategy points that improve eligibility, funding success, or compliance.

**Length & depth:**
- 2–5 bullets

**Language style:**
- Advisory tone

**Typical content:**
- stacking rules
- eligibility nuances
- what helps determine a suitable applicant
- common examples:
  - secure private investor match early
  - confirm incubator/accelerator referral
  - prepare proof of matching funds
  - ensure expenses start after investment agreement
  - show strong IP and commercialization plan

**AI Rule:** Focus on avoiding eligibility issues.

### 6.2 Process Best Practices

**Aim:** Help applicants navigate the process more effectively.

**Length & depth:**
- 2–5 bullets

**Language style:**
- Practical and strategic

**Typical content:**
- preparation tips (e.g. speak with funder before applying)
- online portal logins required
- application strategy
- supplementary documentation
- prepare pitch deck and financial forecasts
- prepare for investment committee questions
- documentation and reporting post-approval

**AI Rule:** Focus on how to apply better, not eligibility.

### 6.3 Forms

Direct access to application materials. No descriptions, just links.

- Program Website: [URL from source material]
- Program Guidelines: [URL from source material]
- Application Portal: [URL from source material]

**Rules:**
- Use these three labels exactly (Title Case). Do not invent alternative labels (e.g., "FCM Portal," "Eligible Costs Document," "FAQ").
- If a URL is in the source material, include it as a clickable link.
- If a URL is not in the source material, omit that bullet entirely. Do not write "[click here]," "TBD," or any placeholder.
- Do not add new rows beyond these three labels, even if the source material contains additional resources.

**AI Rule:** Exactly three possible rows. Include each only if you have a real URL from the source. No descriptions, no placeholders, no extras.

## Section 7: Application Process

### 7.1 Condensed Grant Steps

1–5 steps in plain business language. Typical flow:

1. Confirm eligibility and fit;
2. Prepare business plan, pitch deck, financials, forecasts, and investment materials;
3. Submit application;
4. Receive investment funding;
5. Report on milestones, expenditures, impact, and growth metrics.

**AI Rule:** Keep generic unless program-specific steps are clearly defined.

### 7.2 Extended Grant Steps

Same 1–5 steps with detail. Keep concise; use bullet points.

For each step, cover relevant items from:
- Pre-application prep work (budget, assessment, pre-eligibility survey)
- Timing context (Step 1 approval within X weeks, then Step 2)
- Documentation and information required
- Obtaining documents like quotes or letters of support
- Post-approval claims and reporting process and frequency

## Type-Specific Reminders

- **Investment Value, not Grant Value.** Investment programs give equity for cash. Use *Investment Value* in Section 3.2.
- **Equity stake matters.** Surface the percentage of equity the program takes when cited.
- **Matching capital is common.** Many programs require co-investment from private investors. Make this prominent in Eligibility and Best Practices.
- **Investment committee review.** Most programs have a formal investment review (often more rigorous than grant review). Surface in Program Details.
- **Milestone reporting is the norm.** Investors track growth post-investment. Reflect this in Program Details.
- **Convertible structures.** Some programs invest via convertible debenture or SAFE rather than direct equity. Surface in Investment Value and Program Details.
