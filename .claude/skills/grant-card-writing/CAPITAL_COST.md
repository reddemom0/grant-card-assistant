# Grant Card Writing — Capital Cost

Format spec for **Capital Cost** grants. Load this sub-skill after detecting that the program funds equipment purchases, facility expansion, retrofits, hardware, vehicles, or major physical asset investments.

This sub-skill assumes the agent has already loaded the parent `OVERVIEW` skill and applied its general rules. Everything here is type-specific.

---

## What Capital Cost Grants Consistently Emphasize

- **Physical assets** — construction, retrofits, equipment, vehicles, facility upgrades, accessibility improvements, energy systems, major technology installations
- **Pre-approval requirement** — most programs require formal approval *before* the applicant spends money
- **Reimbursement-based funding** — funds released after asset is purchased and installed
- **Ownership and retention obligations** — applicants often must retain the asset for a defined period

These themes should appear naturally across the Preview, Grant Overview, Eligible Projects, and Program Details sections.

## Section 2: Preview Description

**Aim:** Explain what capital investment the program supports and why.

**Length & depth:**
- 2–3 lines maximum (1–2 sentences)
- Paragraph format, not bullets
- No dollar amounts, no percentages, no eligibility details, no deadlines, no process, no project examples, no program history, no government commitments

**Language style:**
- Plain business language ("Layman's language")
- Outcome-focused (what the business gets)
- No jargon, no program mechanics

**Typical wording:**
- "supports construction of..."
- "funds purchase and installation of..."
- "helps businesses adopt equipment..."
- "improves accessibility, efficiency, capacity, productivity, or emissions performance"

**Formatting:**
- Single paragraph
- No bullets

**AI Rule:** Describe the asset being funded + the intended operational or public benefit. The Preview answers "what is this program?" — nothing else. If a sentence contains a number, a percentage, a date, or the word "eligible," remove it.

## Section 3: General Requirements

### 3.1 Grant Overview

**Aim:** Expand the Preview by adding the key details an applicant needs to decide if the program is a fit for them. Answers: "What is this program, and is it a fit for me?"

**Length & depth:**
- 3–4 lines maximum (2–4 sentences)
- Includes, in this order, only when relevant:
  - grant value to the applicant
  - contribution percentage
  - expected outcomes of the program
  - who it's for

**Language style:**
- Plain business language ("Layman's language")
- Informational, not instructional
- Not narrative history — do not describe when the program launched, why it was created, or government priorities

**Formatting:**
- Paragraph format
- Followed by "Grant Value" as a separate line or block

**Critical distinction — total program budget vs. grant value:**
- The program's total budget (e.g., "$291 million initiative") is the funding allocated to run the overall program. Rarely useful to an individual applicant.
- The grant value is what an individual applicant can receive (e.g., "up to $100K per project, covering up to 50% of eligible costs").
- Always report grant value to the applicant. Never use total program budget as the headline figure.

**AI Rule:** If a sentence describes program mechanics, history, or government goals rather than what's offered to the applicant, remove it. If the source mentions total program funding, do not lead with that number.

### 3.2 Grant Value

**Aim:** Clearly quantify the financial or in-kind support. Consistent structure across all grant cards.

**Length & depth:**
- As short as possible
- Bullets in this order:
  - % of funding (coverage percentage of eligible costs)
  - maximum funding amount per applicant
  - additional funding for specific project types or applicant groups (if applicable — e.g., underrepresented applicants, Indigenous organizations, rural projects)
  - applicant contribution requirement (if applicable — minimum project size, matching funds, contribution %)
  - stream-specific limits or special caps (if applicable)

**Language style:**
- Numeric and precise
- No explanation unless necessary

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points or short lines
- Separate streams/tiers clearly
- Use consistent wording across cards: "Up to X% of eligible costs," "Up to $X per [project/applicant/year]"

**Critical distinction — program budget vs. applicant value:**
- Report grant value to the applicant, not the program's total budget.
- If the source material only gives a total program budget without per-applicant figures, state that the per-applicant maximum is not specified in source material. Do not divide, estimate, or infer.
- If a value isn't in the source material, omit the bullet. Do not fabricate.

**AI Rule:** Use bullets when there are multiple streams, caps, or cost-share rates. Always distinguish between maximum funding and realistic/average funding when source material provides both.

### 3.3 Turnaround Time

**Aim:** Set expectations on how long approval takes.

**Length & depth:**
- One short line

**Language style:**
- Direct and factual

**Formatting:**
- "Within X weeks/months" format
- "Approximately X months"
- Use "To be confirmed" if date is not known

**AI Rule:** Never explain, just state timing.

## Section 4: Eligibility Criteria

Include each sub-section only if clearly cited in program guidelines or webpage. Never infer ineligibility.

### 4.1 Eligible Applicants

**Aim:** Define who qualifies at a business level.

**Length & depth:**
- 4–8 bullet points
- Include:
  - business type
  - location
  - business registration
  - operational history
  - sector restrictions
  - employee size restrictions
  - industry or strategic focus
  - required plans or certifications

**Language style:**
- Clear, eligibility-driven
- No explanation or examples unless necessary
- Include additional clarifications as a "Note" bullet in *italics*

**Formatting:**
- Bullet points ending with semicolons
- Group logically (core eligibility → additional criteria → priorities)

**AI Rule:** Only include criteria about the applicant, not the project. Focus on the applicant's ability to own, operate, maintain, or use the capital asset.

### 4.2 Ineligible Applicants

Same aim, structure, and formatting as Eligible Applicants. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.3 Eligible Projects

**Aim:** Define what types of capital projects qualify.

**Length & depth:**
- 5–10 bullets
- Often grouped by stream

**Language style:**
- Technical but clear

**Formatting:**
- Start with high-level criteria, then examples

**Common project types:**
- new construction
- facility expansion
- building retrofits
- equipment purchases
- accessibility upgrades
- energy efficiency retrofits
- vehicle or fleet equipment
- automation and robotics
- renewable energy systems

**AI Rule:** This section should answer: *"What kind of project can be funded?"*

### 4.4 Ineligible Projects

Same structure, language, and formatting as Eligible Projects. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.5 Eligible Activities

**Aim:** Describe the work involved in completing the capital project.

**Length & depth:**
- 5–8 bullets
- Moderate length (not exhaustive unless necessary)
- Include additional clarifications as a "Note" bullet in *italics*

**Language style:**
- Functional verbs: developing, assessing, implementing
- Activities consistently include:
  - purchase and install equipment
  - retrofit buildings
  - commission new systems, software, and related hardware
  - install accessibility devices
  - implement automation
  - install renewable energy systems

**Formatting:**
- Group by theme if complex

**AI Rule:** Activities describe what the applicant *does*. Projects describe what is being *built, purchased, or upgraded*. Keep them separate.

### 4.6 Ineligible Activities

Same aim, structure, and formatting as Eligible Activities. Include only if clearly cited.

### 4.7 Eligible Expenses

**Aim:** Define what costs are covered.

**Length & depth:**
- Bullet points
- More detailed than activities

**Language style:**
- Specific and practical

**Formatting:**
- Include examples where helpful

**AI Rule:** Eligible expenses must be directly tied to the capital asset or installation.

### 4.8 Ineligible Expenses

Same structure and formatting as Eligible Expenses. Include only if clearly cited.

## Section 7: Application Process

### 7.1 Condensed Grant Steps

1–5 steps in plain business language. Typical flow:

1. Confirm eligibility and project stream;
2. Prepare project plan, quotes, budget, and required technical documents;
3. Submit application;
4. Receive approval before starting work;
5. Complete project and submit claim documents for reimbursement.

**AI Rule:** Keep generic unless program-specific steps are clearly defined.

### 7.2 Extended Grant Steps

Same 1–5 steps with detail. Keep concise; use bullet points.

For each step, cover relevant items from:
- Pre-application prep work (budget, assessment, pre-eligibility survey)
- Timing context (Step 1 approval within X weeks, then Step 2)
- Documentation and information required
- Obtaining documents like quotes or letters of support
- Post-approval claims and reporting process and frequency

## Type-Specific Reminders

- **Pre-approval is critical.** Most Capital Cost programs require formal approval before the applicant purchases the asset. Spending before approval typically disqualifies the expense. Surface this in Program Details and Application Process.
- **Ownership and retention.** Many programs require the applicant to retain the funded asset for a defined period (often 3–5 years). Surface this when cited.
- **Reimbursement-based payment.** Funds released after asset is purchased and installed. Make this prominent in Program Details.
- **Quotes and documentation.** Programs typically require multiple quotes for major purchases, plus invoices and proof of payment for claims. Reflect this in Best Practices and Application Process.
- **Projects vs. activities are distinct.** Eligible Projects = what's being built or purchased. Eligible Activities = what the applicant does to deliver it. Never blur the line.
