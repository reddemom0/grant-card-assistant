# Granted Insights — Hiring Grants

Load this when the program is a Pure Hiring grant or a Hiring-dominant Hybrid (the hiring component is the primary value).

## Reasoning scaffold (internal — do not output)

Work through these before drafting:

1. What should an employer realistically expect from this program — wage subsidy, full salary coverage, partial reimbursement?
2. What type of hire is most likely to qualify? Net-new only? Specific demographics, age bands, equity groups, education status?
3. Does timing matter — must the application precede the offer, the start date, or both?
4. How administratively heavy is this for the employer — payroll reporting, retention requirements, milestone check-ins?
5. How competitive or restrictive does the program appear? Are intake windows narrow? Is funding capped per employer?
6. What employer profile is a strong fit vs. a weak fit?
7. What are the top 3 things to tell a client before they spend time on this?
8. What expectation should we set on timeline, paperwork, and likelihood of fit?

## Pay special attention to

- **Net-new hiring requirement.** Pure Hiring programs almost always require the placement to be beyond the employer's baseline headcount. Backfilling a departed employee usually does not qualify.
- **Candidate demographic constraints.** Age bands (under 30, under 29), equity-group targeting (Indigenous, women in STEM, persons with disabilities, newcomers), student/recent-graduate status. Misreading these wastes consultant time.
- **Timing rules.** Many programs require the application or approval to precede the offer or start date. Retroactive applications often disqualify.
- **Reimbursement structure.** Wage subsidies are typically reimbursed after payroll is run and reported. Cash flow burden is real for small employers.
- **Employer eligibility.** Some programs exclude federal contractors, regulated industries, or businesses below a revenue/employee threshold.
- **Reporting burden.** Quarterly or milestone-based payroll reporting is common. Some programs require retention proof (employee still on staff at 6 or 12 months).

## Output format

```
## Granted Insights — Hiring Grant

**Top 3 insights**
1. [Most important strategic point]
2. [Second insight]
3. [Third insight]

**Best-fit employer**
[1–2 sentences on the employer profile and hire profile that has a real shot]

**Effort level**
[Low / Moderate / High — one sentence on what drives it, typically payroll reporting + candidate documentation]

**Competitiveness / restrictiveness**
[Assessment. For hiring programs, "restrictiveness" is often the bigger factor than competition — say so if the constraints narrow the pool significantly.]

**Key watchouts**
- [Watchout 1 — typically net-new, candidate eligibility, or timing]
- [Watchout 2]
- [Watchout 3 if material]

**Expectation to set with client**
[2–3 sentences. Cover candidate constraint tightness, whether timing forces them to delay hiring, and whether the reimbursement is meaningful relative to administrative effort.]
```
