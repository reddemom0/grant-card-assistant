# Granted Insights — Voice Exemplar

This file shows what a complete insights output looks like in the target voice. Read it before drafting. The format adjusts per grant type (see the loaded type-specific sub-skill for section names), but the voice and rhythm stay the same across all types.

## Example: BC Workforce Development Agreement — Employer Training Grant (Hiring Stream)

```
## Granted Insights — Hiring Grant

**Top 3 insights**

1. The 4-6 week approval window is the killer. Any wages paid before approval don't count. If a client's already extended an offer or started someone, they're locked out. We need to be in the conversation before they hire.

2. Budget burns down fast. Last year intake closed in October after four months. "Rolling intake" is misleading. Apply in Q1, or you're rolling the dice.

3. Priority hires get 80% reimbursement, general hires get 60%. At the $10K cap that's a $2K swing per hire, which matters a lot for employers hiring in volume. Self-identification has to be genuine and documented.

**Best-fit employer**

Growth-stage BC business that's planning headcount expansion, especially in roles that pull from younger candidates, newcomers, or persons with disabilities. Trades, hospitality, entry-level tech, healthcare support. Annual revenue over $50K, and patience for the 4-6 week wait between application and offer.

**Effort level**

High, mostly on the back end. The application itself is manageable. What gets you is what comes after: quarterly payroll attestations for twelve months, an annual training completion report, retention confirmation at month 12, and a 60-day claim deadline on every expense. Multiple hires through this program means an ongoing reporting workflow.

**Competitiveness / restrictiveness**

Restrictiveness more than competition. Historical approval rate sits around 68%, so once an application's in, the bar isn't brutal. What narrows the funnel is timing (budget exhaustion) and candidate eligibility rules. The net-new requirement, post-approval hire timing, and 12-month prior-employment exclusion cut out a lot of candidates. For a client wanting to backfill or convert a contractor, this is dead on arrival.

**Key watchouts**

- Retroactive wages are out. Any wages paid before written approval can't be claimed. No exceptions. Factor the 4-6 week approval window into the client's hiring timeline.
- Clawback if an employee leaves within 6 months for "employer-attributable reasons." That phrase is fuzzy and could be invoked in a dispute. For clients in hospitality, seasonal work, or commission-heavy sales, take the clawback seriously.
- The 12-month retention requirement runs past the 26-week wage subsidy. So a client could finish collecting the subsidy and still owe a clawback if the employee leaves between months 6 and 12.
- Commission-only and under-30-hour positions don't qualify. Hits sales-heavy or part-time-dependent employers.

**Expectation to set with client**

Solid money when it works. $10K per hire with 80% reimbursement for priority hires is a strong return for an employer who plans ahead. But this isn't reactive funding. They need 4-6 weeks of patience between application and bringing someone on, and 12 months of reporting per hire. And the calendar matters. Last year the budget was gone by October. Tell them: if you want this in the current fiscal year, we apply now. After their next hiring decision, it's too late.
```

---

## Why this works

Patterns to notice and reproduce:

- **Sentence length varies aggressively.** Short punches ("Solid money when it works.") sit next to longer thoughts. No three-in-a-row of similar length.
- **Contractions throughout.** Don't, isn't, they're, what's, client's, application's. Their absence is the single fastest way to sound like AI.
- **No contrastive negation as rhetorical filler.** When opposites appear ("before approval / after approval"), they're carrying real information. No "X, not Y" used as a pivot.
- **Specifics replace abstraction.** "Last year intake closed in October after four months" beats "the program closes early." Concrete numbers and dates beat generic claims.
- **No telegraphing.** No "the single biggest risk," no "the real constraint." The insights land on their own weight.
- **Direct advice in the closing.** "Tell them: we apply now" reads like a strategist briefing a colleague, not a deck summarizing what the reader just read.
