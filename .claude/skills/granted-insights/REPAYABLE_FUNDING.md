# Granted Insights — Repayable Funding, Loans, and Non-Dilutive Financing

Load for any program where the primary funding mechanism is repayable — commercial loans, forgivable loans, mixed grant/loan programs, and non-dilutive financing.

## Reasoning scaffold (internal — do not output)

1. What should a business realistically expect — flexible growth capital, project-based financing, working capital support, asset financing, or something narrower?
2. What stage / sector / business profile is the program designed for? Established companies only, or earlier-stage realistic?
3. What are the financial tradeoffs — repayment terms, interest rate, security, matching requirements, restrictions on use of funds? Does this behave more like a grant substitute, a traditional loan, or something in between?
4. How hard does this look to secure? Relationship-driven, diligence-heavy, financially selective, operationally burdensome?
5. What preparation is required — financial statements, forecasts, repayment plan, business plan, quotes, traction evidence?
6. What are the biggest watchouts — repayment timing, restricted use of funds, personal/corporate guarantees, reporting burden, slow timelines, cash flow mismatch?
7. Is anything in the description easy to misunderstand — sounds like grant funding but is repayable, sounds broad but narrowly scoped?
8. What expectation should we set?

## Pay special attention to

- **Forgivable portions.** Many programs are partly forgivable if conditions are met (job creation, milestone hit, retention period). The forgivable portion is the real "grant equivalent." Quantify it for the client.
- **Underwriting and due diligence.** Loan programs require financial review the applicant must prepare for — historical statements, forecasts, debt schedule. Treat this as table stakes.
- **Personal guarantees and collateral.** Common in commercial loan programs but not universal. Material to the client's decision. Flag only if cited in source.
- **"Loans require more proof than grants."** Documentation quality is a bigger competitive lever here than in grant programs. Weak financials disqualify even a strong project narrative.
- **Repayment timing.** Some programs defer repayment 1–2 years; others start immediately on disbursement. Cash flow alignment is critical.
- **Interest rate vs. market.** Below-market rates make a program attractive even when repayable. Above-market or market-rate programs need to clear a higher bar than just access to capital.
- **Restricted use of funds.** Working capital, equipment, real estate, R&D — programs typically restrict to one or two categories. Mismatched use disqualifies.
- **"Sounds like a grant" trap.** Some repayable programs are marketed in grant-like language. The client may not understand it's debt until late in the conversation. Surface this early.
- **Application burden vs. funding amount.** Loan applications can rival commercial bank diligence. If the program's funding is modest, the burden may not justify the effort.
