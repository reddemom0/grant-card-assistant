# Granted Insights — Market Expansion Grants

Load for export development, trade missions, distributor search, market entry, international marketing, and related programs.

## Reasoning scaffold (internal — do not output)

1. What should a business realistically expect — reimbursement on travel/marketing/trade show costs, capped per market, capped per applicant?
2. What kinds of expansion activity are most aligned — trade shows, export development, distributor search, market entry, marketing assets, travel?
3. What company profile is a strong fit — export readiness already required, or is this designed for first-time exporters?
4. How much planning and documentation does a business need before applying — export plan, target market analysis, quotes for activities?
5. Is this a clean reimbursement opportunity or does it require significant strategy and prep?
6. What are the practical watchouts — eligible markets, project windows, cost categories, reimbursement rules?
7. How competitive does it appear?
8. What are the top 3 things to tell a client?

## Pay special attention to

- **Export readiness threshold.** Some programs require demonstrated export readiness (existing international sales, an export plan, language capability). First-time exporters often do not qualify despite the marketing suggesting otherwise.
- **Eligible markets.** Domestic vs. international is the first question. Within international, many programs exclude specific markets (e.g., U.S. only, or specifically not U.S.). Confirm before recommending.
- **Project window.** Most programs require the activity to occur within a strict window — often a fiscal year or a defined project period. Activities outside the window are ineligible regardless of fit.
- **Reimbursement is the norm.** Applicants spend first, claim later. Cash flow burden is meaningful at the upper funding tiers.
- **Cost category exclusions.** Salaries, overhead, inventory, and product development are usually excluded. Eligible costs are typically travel, booth fees, marketing assets, market research, and translation.
- **Cost-share expectations.** Most programs reimburse 50–75%. Applicants need to fund the balance.
- **Streams matter.** Many programs have planning vs. implementation vs. trade show streams with different caps. Misreading the stream wastes time.
- **Project quality bar.** Programs increasingly want a real strategy — target market rationale, buyer research, partner identification — not just "we want to go to a trade show."

## Output format

```
## Granted Insights — Market Expansion Grant

**Top 3 insights**
1. [Most important strategic point]
2. [Second insight]
3. [Third insight]

**Best-fit company / expansion plan**
[1–2 sentences on company profile (export readiness, sector, size) and the expansion plan shape that actually fits]

**Effort level**
[Low / Moderate / High — one sentence. Often Moderate-High due to strategy documentation burden.]

**Competitiveness**
[Assessment. Note if competitiveness is driven by readiness bar rather than oversubscription.]

**Key watchouts**
- [Watchout 1 — typically eligible markets, project window, or readiness bar]
- [Watchout 2]
- [Watchout 3 if material]

**Expectation to set with client**
[2–3 sentences. Cover whether they're export-ready in the program's sense, whether their target market is eligible, and the spend-first cash flow reality.]
```
